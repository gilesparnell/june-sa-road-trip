Day 2 · for the boys

Rest day, fam — no driving, just pure vibes. Run around the farm, light a fire, eat boerewors like an absolute unit. If you've got the aura for it, there's ONE optional adventure: Naude's Nek (highest road pass in SA — it's giving Mordor, no cap), Rhodes trout fishing, or Tiffindell ski resort. Pick one — not all three.

The Point of Today

Don't over-programme it

Day 1 is an 8-hour push and Day 3 is another 5-hour leg, so Friday is deliberately empty — a buffer day. The most valuable thing it gives you is time with family at Funny Stone, which is the entire reason for the route's first half. Late breakfast, walk the farm, kids loose in the veld, fire by mid-afternoon. The Funny Stone elders aren't getting any younger; the boys will remember a slow Friday more than another viewpoint.

That said — if you do want one outing, the Barkly East area is one of the most under-rated mountain destinations in South Africa, and you're already in it. Pick one, not all four. Save the rest for next time.

★ Excursions from Funny Stone

Four options — pick one

Each is a half-day return trip from Barkly East. The first three are weather-dependent; the last one is fine in any conditions.

Option A — Naude's Nek summit drive

Iconic

The highest road pass in SA at 2,587m — gravel road over the Drakensberg, dropping into the Tina valley and Rhodes village. Drive to the summit and turn back (3-hour round trip) — don't attempt the full traverse with kids. Snow common in June; check conditions the night before. The hairpins, the cliff drops, the sheer scale — awe-inspiring for ten-year-olds.

Option B — Rhodes village & trout fishing

Kid magnet

Rhodes is a tiny historic village 40 min north of Barkly East, tucked at the base of the Drakensberg. Walkerbouts Inn rents kids' rods; the Sterkstroom River is stocked with rainbow trout. No licence needed for juniors. Wood-fired pizza or pancakes at Walkerbouts for lunch.

Option C — Tiffindell & Ben McDhui

Status varies

South Africa's only ski resort at 2,720m on Ben McDhui. Operations are intermittent — call ahead. If the tow is running: two-hour lift pass, a few runs, skiing in Africa. If not: drive to the summit plateau (3,000m) and let the boys play in snow patches.

Option D — Barkly East railway zigzags

Engineering curiosity

Just outside town: the disused Stormberg–Barkly East railway uses eight reverses (zigzags) to climb the escarpment — one of only a handful of zigzag-reverse railway systems ever built. Park at the lower reverse and walk a section. Closed shoes — rocky underfoot. Quick excursion with snacks and a flask.

💰
Free

Practical: Whatever you pick, be back at Funny Stone by 4pm. Fire by 4:30, dinner at 6, kids in bed by 8 — Day 3 is a 5-hour drive starting at 7am Saturday morning.

Same farm as last night — Robert and Jessamy Viedge's place outside Barkly East. No packing, no moving; the whole point of the rest day is sitting with the extended family on a farm of exquisite beauty. Make a proper braai, dinner outside if it's clear, fire inside if it's not.

🧒 For the boys

Day 2 highlights for ten-year-olds

  • Naude's Nek summit — at 2,587m, highest road pass in SA. Possible snow. Awesome scale.
  • Rhodes trout fishing — stocked river, rods hired, kids will catch fish.
  • Ben McDhui — SA's second-highest peak. If snow, sledding. If not, snowball-fight-worthy patches.
  • Barkly East rail zigzags — once they grasp why the engineers had to zigzag, this gets surprisingly engaging.
  • Farm time — running loose in the veld is the real win of the day.
