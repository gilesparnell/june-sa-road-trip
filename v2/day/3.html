The Drive

One of SA's most scenic 360 km

A long day but a rewarding one — the route change makes the difference. After Aliwal North, forget the flat inland road to Bethlehem: take the Maloti Route (R26) east, which follows the Lesotho border through the sandstone foothills. The Maluti Mountains are with you for 120 km — pink and orange cliffs with snow on the higher peaks in June. End with the spectacular R711 into Clarens. Leave Funny Stone by 7am and you'll arrive in Clarens around noon to 1pm.

Route waypoints

Maloti Route via Fouriesburg

~07:50

Barkly East → Lady Grey

~65 km, 50 min. Foothills road through the Klein Berg. Lady Grey is named after the wife of British Governor Sir George Grey — the colonial naming of the Eastern Cape is impossible to miss through here.

~08:50

Lady Grey → Aliwal North

~50 km, 45 min. Crosses into the Free State at the Orange River. Fuel and food stop. The town has Boer War blockhouse ruins and was the site of a British concentration camp during the Second Boer War.

~10:00

Aliwal North → Zastron

~85 km, 1 hr. Now on the Maloti Route (R26), hugging the Lesotho border foothills. The Maluti Mountains begin to appear on your right — sandstone massifs that shift from pink to orange to gold. This is the turn that makes Day 3 special.

~11:00

Zastron → Fouriesburg (R26)

~120 km, 1.75 hrs. One of the most scenic drives in South Africa. Maluti Mountains a constant companion — snow-capped in June. Fouriesburg was the wartime capital of the Orange Free State Republic after Bloemfontein fell in March 1900.

~12:45

Fouriesburg → Clarens (R711)

~25 km, 20 min. Save this for the afternoon light. The R711 climbs through extraordinary rock formations, views over the Caledon River valley, snow-capped Maloti peaks on the horizon. One of the most beautiful short drives in SA.

Historical note — Fouriesburg: Fouriesburg was the provisional capital of the Orange Free State after Bloemfontein fell to Lord Roberts. In the surrounding mountains, 4,300 Boer fighters were trapped and captured at Surrender Hill on 30 July 1900 — the biggest single capture of the entire Anglo-Boer War. Christiaan de Wet, who escaped through Golden Gate with a small force the night before, called the surrender "a horrible murder of government, country and people."

Where you're sleeping

Clarens — two farm-stay options for 6

The Barbet Country Cottages (top pick)

Partly thatched 3-bedroom cottage on a large farm 17 km from Clarens along the scenic Maluti route. Cosy sunroom with fireplace, fully equipped kitchen, sleeps 6+1. Big farm feel, space for the boys to run. ~R655 pp/night (~R3,930/night for 6). Exactly the vibe — thatched, fires, farm.

🧒 For the boys

Day 3 highlights for ten-year-olds

  • Aliwal Hot Springs — 34°C thermal pool in mid-winter.
  • Maluti Mountains — those snow-capped peaks on the left are Lesotho. A whole country next door.
  • Surrender Hill — the open landscape where 4,300 Boer fighters gave up. Imagine that many men in one field.
  • Clarens ice cream & Spruit Walk — Clarens Creamery on the square, stream walk, sandstone cliffs glowing orange in afternoon light. Let them run.
  • Clarens Xtreme zipline — pick one or two activities for the late afternoon if there's energy left.
