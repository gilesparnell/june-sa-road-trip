The Drive

The new longer day

Because we're skipping Wakkerstroom, this is the leg that absorbs the extra distance: Clarens out via Bethlehem, north through Reitz and Frankfort, across the highveld to Standerton, then east on the R23/R36 through Carolina and up into Dullstroom. Not the most dramatic stretch — the Free State maize country is wide open, agricultural, and quiet — but it moves well, the surface is good throughout, and a Monday in mid-winter means minimal traffic. Fuel-and-coffee in Bethlehem, lunch in Standerton, arrive Dullstroom mid-afternoon. Full evening to settle in.

Route waypoints

Just keep moving

~08:30

Clarens → Bethlehem

~35 km, 35 min. Easy warm-up on the R711/N5. First proper town — 24-hour fuel, Wimpy / Mugg & Bean for second breakfast, public toilets. Stock up on water and snacks — the next 200 km are mostly farm roads.

~09:30

Bethlehem → Reitz → Frankfort

~120 km, 1.5 hrs. North on the R26 through classic Free State maize country. Reitz was once Christiaan de Wet's home town. The road is straight, the horizon enormous, the kids can play "spot the windpomp" — there are hundreds.

~11:30

Frankfort → Standerton

~95 km, 1 hr. North on R26 then east on R23 across the Vaal River into Mpumalanga. Point out the Vaal crossing to the boys — the historic boundary between the Free State and the old Transvaal. Standerton is fuel + toilets + lunch (Spur on the main road is reliable).

~13:30

Standerton → Carolina (R36 / R39)

~110 km, 1.25 hrs. East through highland farmland. Carolina was a Boer War garrison town; the British built blockhouses through here in 1901 to choke de Wet's commando supply lines.

~14:45

Carolina → Dullstroom (R540)

~75 km, 1 hr. Final climb into the highlands. Dullstroom sits at 2,100m — expect cold, clear air and possibly frost on the dam edges by parking time.

This is the day to keep moving. No big detours, no Panorama-style stops — Bethlehem coffee, Standerton lunch, Carolina fuel, then push to Dullstroom. The reward is a long peaceful afternoon and evening at a trout farm. Save the dawdling for Day 5.

Where you're sleeping

Dullstroom — two farm-stay options

Blue Crane Farm (top pick — boys will love it)

Three 6-sleeper units (Otters Cove, Misty Waters, Weavers Nest) just 50m from the water's edge of a 9-hectare trout dam. Multiple fishing jetties, beginner-friendly fly fishing, full self-catering with fireplaces. ~R4,200/night for 6.

bluecranefarm.co.za →

Millstream Farm

3 km from Dullstroom village. Spacious cottages sleeping up to 10, each with full kitchen, lounge with fireplace, private patio with braai overlooking the trout dams. From ~R2,820/night. SA's premier fly-fishing destination.

🧒 For the boys

Day 4 highlights for ten-year-olds

  • Vaal River crossing — historic boundary between the old Free State and the old Transvaal. A whole-province "where are we now" moment from the bridge.
  • Windpomp counting — endless wind-pump water pumps across the maize country. 50 = R10 ice cream at the Spar in Bethlehem.
  • Dullstroom Birds of Prey — eagles, owls, vultures up close. Fri–Mon only.
  • Trout-dam jetties at Blue Crane Farm — rods for hire, fish are biting, the boys can throw sticks in the water while supper cooks.
  • Anvil Ale / Wally's — kid-safe gardens; parents earned a beer.
