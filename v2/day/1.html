The Drive

8 hours up the spine of the Eastern Cape

Leave Kenton by 7am — this is the longest driving day of the trip. Pick up the hire car in Grahamstown, then head north-west on the R67 to Fort Beaufort, north through Adelaide to Queenstown, then south-east through Sterkstroom and Dordrecht as the road climbs steadily into the Berg foothills. Barkly East sits at 1,700m in a spectacular highland valley. Leaving at 7am puts you there around 3pm.

⚠️ Do NOT take the Bedford road (R350). Chronically in poor condition. Take the R67 to Fort Beaufort instead — fully resurfaced 2020. Adds ~30 min vs Bedford but a completely different driving experience.

Route waypoints

Take Fort Beaufort, not Bedford

~07:30

Kenton-on-Sea → Grahamstown (Makhanda)

~80 km, 1 hr. Pick up hire car, fill the tank, big shop for the Funny Stone days. The R67 starts north out of town.

~09:00

Grahamstown → Fort Beaufort (R67)

70 km, 1 hr. R67 (the Queen's Road) only — do NOT take the Bedford road. Well-maintained resurfaced tar through Ecca Pass (Ecca shales, 250 million years old) into frontier-fort country.

~10:30

Fort Beaufort → Queenstown (Komani)

140 km, 1.75 hrs. Crosses Nico Malan Pass — 673m climb over 13.8 km, one of SA's top 25 altitude-gaining passes. Good fuel & coffee stop in Queenstown before the highlands.

~12:30

Queenstown → Dordrecht

100 km, 1.25 hrs. The road begins to climb into the Berg foothills; landscape changes noticeably.

~14:00

Dordrecht → Barkly East

60 km, 1 hr. Increasingly spectacular as you enter the highlands at 1,700m. Arrive Funny Stone ~15:00.

Historical context: The R67 you're driving was built by the British army as a strategic road between the Grahamstown garrison and Fort Beaufort. They called it The Queen's Road. Nine Cape Frontier Wars (1779–1878) were fought across this country — the Eastern Cape was contested territory for almost a century.

★ Activities possible along the way

Day 1 is mostly driving — pick one short stop

An 8-hour day doesn't leave much room. These are quick (20–30 min) historical stops you can fold in around lunch or a leg-stretch.

Observatory Museum — Camera Obscura (Makhanda)

Kid magnet

Victorian 1850 house with a working camera obscura on the roof — projects a live image of Makhanda onto a parabolic dish. A genuine "wow" stop. 30 minutes on Bathurst Street.

Martello Tower (Fort Beaufort)

Roadside

British military gun tower from 1837 — a national monument, one of only two in the world with a full-swivel 360° cannon. Visible from the R67 as you pass through Fort Beaufort. Walk-around outside is free.

Queenstown lunch break

Pragmatic

Not really an "activity" — but Queenstown (Komani) is the most sensible lunch + toilets + tank-top-up halfway point on the day. Wimpy / Spur on the N6 strip are reliable.

Where you're sleeping

Funny Stone — two nights

Funny Stone (family)

Family property in Barkly East. Arrive Friday, unpack, light a fire, sleep deeply. Saturday is a deliberate rest day — see Day 2 for excursion options. You're here for two nights before pushing on to Clarens Sunday morning.

If you arrive with energy

  • Walk the farm — the boys will want to explore before dark
  • Barkly East village has a good butchery — grab boerewors for the braai
  • Sunset at altitude hits fast in June — fire on by 4:30pm
🧒 For the boys

Day 1 highlights for ten-year-olds

  • Camera obscura at the Observatory Museum — live "magic" projection of the town on the rooftop dish.
  • Ecca Pass shales — 250-million-year-old rock layers in the cuttings, 15 km north of Grahamstown. Geology you can see from the car.
  • Nico Malan Pass — 673m climb in 13.8 km. Watch the windows steam up as you climb.
  • Altitude shift — Barkly East is at 1,700m. Likely cold, possibly frosty. Compare with sea-level Kenton this morning.
