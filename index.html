Trip overview

A 5-day road trip from the Eastern Cape coast up to Hoedspruit, designed around rural farm stays, kid-friendly outdoor adventure, and avoiding Johannesburg entirely. The route hand-picks five very different landscapes: coastal Eastern Cape, mistbelt Berg foothills, Free State sandstone country, the highveld birding villages, the Mpumalanga trout highlands, and the Panorama Route's spectacular cliffs.

A note on driving times: All times shown are Google Maps drive time plus 20% to account for toilet stops, fuel, and snack breaks — essential with kids on board. For example, Google Maps shows Day 2 (Barkly East → Clarens) as 5h20m; we're showing 6.5h. Plan on a comfort stop roughly every 90 minutes.

Three things to know going in:

  • Day 1 is the long day (Kenton → Barkly East, 8h) — leave by 7am. Day 2 is 5h (Barkly East → Clarens via Maloti Route). Days 3 and 4 are 5h each. Day 5 is ~3.5h driving broken up by Panorama Route stops.
  • June nights inland are COLD (often below freezing in the Berg, Free State, and Mpumalanga highlands). Every recommended stay has a working fireplace — bring beanies, gloves, and a thermos.
  • Self-catering throughout means you can leave at dawn without waiting for breakfast. Pre-pack travel mugs, fruit, and rusks for the road.

Day 1 Friday 19 June

Kenton-on-Sea → Barkly East (Funny Stone) [~468 km]

~468 km 8 hours driving ⚠ Leave Kenton by 7am

The drive

Leave Kenton by 7am — this is an 8h day. Pick up the hire car in Grahamstown, then head north-west on the R67 to Fort Beaufort, north through Adelaide to Queenstown, then south-east through Sterkstroom and Dordrecht as the road climbs steadily into the Berg foothills. Barkly East sits at 1,700m in a spectacular highland valley. Leaving at 7am puts you there around 3pm.

What you'll see along the way

  • Ecca Pass (15 km north of Grahamstown) — tarred, built by road pioneer Andrew Geddes Bain in the 1800s. Beautiful geological formations — the Ecca shales are 250 million years old. A gentle warm-up pass before the day gets going.
  • Grahamstown (Makhanda) — the 1820 British Settlers landed on this frontier. The Battle of Grahamstown (1819) — where 10,000 Xhosa warriors attacked a British garrison of fewer than 400 — defined the colonial frontier. The whole Eastern Cape was contested territory for almost a century (nine Cape Frontier Wars, 1779–1878).
  • Fort Beaufort — founded in 1822 as a British military frontier post; the British army was here until 1870. The Martello Tower (a national monument — one of only two in the world with a full-swivel 360° cannon) is visible from the road. The War of the Axe (7th Frontier War, 1846) started here — sparked by the rescue of a prisoner being transferred to Grahamstown. The R67 you're driving was literally built by the British military as a strategic road from Grahamstown headquarters to the fort. They called it The Queen's Road.
  • Nico Malan Pass (Fort Beaufort → Whittlesea) — ranked in the top 25 altitude-gaining passes in South Africa. 673 vertical metres over 13.8 km — forested sections, steep cuttings, sweeping views, nicely banked corners. Road-trip forums consistently call this a joy to drive. Upgraded 2016.
⚠️ Do NOT take the Bedford road (R350). The road between Grahamstown and Bedford is chronically in poor condition — widely reported as undrivable. Take the R67 to Fort Beaufort instead. It was fully resurfaced in a R226 million SANRAL project (completed 2020): 70 km of new tar, widened shoulders. Adds ~30 min vs Bedford but is a completely different driving experience.
📍 Open Day 1 route in Google Maps

Route waypoints — take Fort Beaufort, not Bedford

  • Kenton-on-Sea → Grahamstown (Makhanda) — ~80 km, 1 hr — pick up hire car, fill the tank, big shop for Funny Stone days
  • Grahamstown → Fort Beaufort — 70 km, 1 hr — R67 (The Queen's Road) only. Do not take the Bedford road. The R67 is well-maintained resurfaced tar through the Ecca Pass (Ecca shales, 250 million years old) into frontier fort country
  • Fort Beaufort → Queenstown (Komani) — 140 km, 1.75 hrs — crosses Nico Malan Pass (673m climb over 13.8 km — one of SA's top 25 altitude-gaining passes). Good fuel & coffee stop in Queenstown before heading into the highlands
  • Queenstown → Dordrecht — 100 km, 1.25 hrs — road begins to climb into the Berg foothills; landscape changes noticeably
  • Dordrecht → Barkly East — 60 km, 1 hr — increasingly spectacular as you enter the highlands at 1,700m

Where you're staying — Funny Stone

Funny Stone (family)

Family property in Barkly East. Arrive, unpack, light a fire. You've earned it — the rest of the trip is from here.

If you arrive with energy

  • Walk the farm — the boys will want to explore before dark
  • Barkly East village is small but has a good butchery — grab boerewors for the braai
  • Sunset at altitude hits fast in June — fire on by 4:30pm

Day 2 Saturday 20 June

Barkly East → Clarens (Eastern Free State) [~360 km]

~360 km 5 hours driving ⚠ Leave Funny Stone by 7am

The drive

A long day but a rewarding one — the route change makes the difference. After Aliwal North, forget the flat inland road to Bethlehem: take the Maloti Route (Route 26) east, which follows the Lesotho border through the sandstone foothills. The Maluti Mountains are with you for 120 km — pink and orange cliffs with snow on the higher peaks in June. End with the spectacular R711 into Clarens. Leave Funny Stone by 7am and you'll arrive in Clarens around noon to 1pm.

📍 Open Day 2 route in Google Maps (Maloti Route via Fouriesburg)

Route waypoints (approximate — total ~360 km)

  • Barkly East → Lady Grey — ~65 km, 50 min — lovely foothills road through the Klein Berg. Lady Grey is named after the wife of British Governor Sir George Grey — the British colonial naming of the Eastern Cape is impossible to miss through here.
  • Lady Grey → Aliwal North — ~50 km, 45 min — crosses into the Free State at the Orange River. Fuel and food stop in Aliwal North. The town has Boer War blockhouse ruins and was the site of a British concentration camp during the Second Boer War.
  • Aliwal North → Zastron — ~85 km, 1 hr — now on the Maloti Route (Route 26), hugging the Lesotho border foothills. The Maluti Mountains begin to appear on your right — sandstone massifs that shift from pink to orange to gold as the light changes. This is the turn that makes Day 2 special.
  • Zastron → Fouriesburg (Route 26, Maloti Route) — ~120 km, 1.75 hrs — one of the most scenic drives in South Africa. The Maluti Mountains are a constant companion along the Lesotho border — snow-capped in June. Fouriesburg was the last capital of the Orange Free State during the Anglo-Boer War. After Bloemfontein fell to the British in March 1900, the Boer government relocated here. It didn't last: on 30 July 1900, General Marthinus Prinsloo surrendered 4,300 Boer troops at nearby Surrender Hill — the largest Boer capture of the entire war. You'll drive through this country.
  • Fouriesburg → Clarens (R711) — ~25 km, 20 min — save this last bit for the afternoon light. The R711 climbs through extraordinary rock formations, views over the Caledon River valley, snow-capped Maloti peaks on the horizon. This 25km stretch is consistently called one of the most beautiful short drives in South Africa.

Historical note — Fouriesburg: Fouriesburg was the provisional capital of the Orange Free State Republic after Bloemfontein fell to Lord Roberts in March 1900. In the surrounding mountains, 4,300 Boer fighters were trapped and captured at "Surrender Hill" on 30 July 1900 — the biggest single capture of the entire Anglo-Boer War. Christiaan de Wet, who escaped through the Golden Gate with a small force the night before, called the surrender "a horrible murder of government, country and people." The town you drive through carries all of this history quietly.

budget-getaways.co.za — Schaapplaats →

If you arrive with energy

  • Spruit Walk (Lake Clarens) — easy 30–45 min family trail, perfect leg-stretcher after a long day in the car
  • Clarens Brewery for the adults, ice cream at the square for the boys
  • Sunset on the koppies behind the village — sandstone glows golden in the last light

Day 3 Sunday 21 June

Clarens → Wakkerstroom (Mpumalanga) [~235 km]

~235 km 5 hours driving Arrive mid-afternoon

The drive

A shorter day and the most dramatic scenery before the Panorama Route. Leave Clarens on the R712 east — within 20 minutes you are inside Golden Gate Highlands National Park, one of the most spectacular drives in the Free State. After Harrismith, the road opens into highveld before dropping into Wakkerstroom. Leave at 9am and arrive comfortably by 2–3pm.

📍 Open Day 3 route in Google Maps (via Golden Gate)

Route waypoints (approximate — total ~235 km)

  • Clarens → Golden Gate National Park (R712 east) — ~20 km to park entrance, ~45 km through the park — fully tarred since 2018 (previously gravel). Road-trippers consistently call this "the most scenic drive in the Free State." The road climbs through Lichens Pass at 2,041m — sandstone cliffs tower on both sides, turning gold as the sun moves. The park charges a SANParks conservation levy (~R220/person) — budget for this.
  • Golden Gate → Harrismith — ~55 km, 45 min — descend from the escarpment. Harrismith was the last significant town held by Boer forces in the Free State — when it fell to the British in August 1900, the entire Boer supply chain through Van Reenen's Pass to Natal opened up. The town still has blockhouses and a military cemetery from the war.
  • Harrismith → Volksrust (N11) — ~110 km, 1.25 hrs — good fast road across the highveld.
  • Volksrust → Wakkerstroom (R543, Wakkerstroomnek Pass) — ~24 km, 25 min — a gentle tarred pass, westward views from the summit toward Volksrust. Named Volksrust ("People's Rest") — this was a Boer stronghold and a natural staging point for commandos moving through the Mpumalanga highlands during the war.

Historical note — Golden Gate and the Brandwater Basin: In July 1900, the mountains you're driving through formed a trap. The British surrounded 4,300 Boer troops in the Brandwater Basin (Fouriesburg area), blocking all the passes including Golden Gate to the east. Christiaan de Wet famously slipped out through Golden Gate with a small commando the night before the mass surrender — one of the great escapes of the war. When you drive through the park on the R712, you are driving through the very pass the British blocked to prevent that escape. Most of the Boers weren't so lucky.

Where to stay

Wakkerstroom self-catering / guest house (book early — limited for 6)

Wakkerstroom is a tiny Victorian-era village — the accommodation pool is small but charming. Look for self-catering cottages that sleep 6 on Airbnb, SafariNow, or wakkerstroom.co.za. The village has a fireplace-and-braai culture; most properties are cosy and well set up for winter. Options include Ingwe Pan and Wakkerstroom Bush Camp.

wakkerstroom.co.za — accommodation →

Book early: Wakkerstroom is a very small town. Self-catering for 6 people is limited — don't leave this to the last minute.

Wakkerstroom highlights

  • Birding — Wakkerstroom is one of South Africa's top birding destinations. Bald Ibis, Blue Crane, and Wattled Crane are common. Even non-birders find it peaceful
  • Victorian village walk — stone buildings, original storefronts, a proper small-town feel. Easy 30-min wander with the kids
  • Evening fire — at 1,600m, June evenings in Wakkerstroom are properly cold and perfectly clear. Big braai, early night, ready for tomorrow's easy drive to Dullstroom

Day 4 Monday 22 June

Wakkerstroom → Dullstroom [~220 km]

~220 km 4 hours driving Easy day — arrive early afternoon

The drive

The easiest driving day of the trip — and shorter than the old Ermelo route. Head north from Wakkerstroom to the tiny village of Amsterdam, then swing east on the R33 through Carolina into the Mpumalanga highlands. Leave at 8am and you're in Dullstroom by noon. The whole afternoon to fish.

Wakkerstroom itself deserves a slow start. The British maintained a besieged garrison here during the Boer War while Boer commandos fired on the town from the surrounding hills for months at a stretch. The morning before you leave is worth a walk through the Victorian village — one of the best-preserved in Mpumalanga. Then head north on quiet tar roads toward Amsterdam.

The R33 from Amsterdam to Carolina runs through a beautiful stretch of Mpumalanga highland farmland — rolling grasslands, dolerite kopjes, and cattle country. It's not dramatic in the Drakensberg sense, but it's peaceful and traffic-free: this is a Monday in mid-winter, not a holiday weekend. Carolina was a Boer War garrison town and still has the bones of that era. From there the R540 climbs steadily into the misty highlands, and Dullstroom appears over a rise at 2,100m.

📍 Open Day 4 route in Google Maps

Route waypoints (approximate — total ~220 km)

  • Wakkerstroom → Amsterdam — ~30 km, 30 min — quick hop north on good tar through open highveld. Amsterdam is a blink-and-miss-it village but worth slowing down for a coffee stop if anything is open.
  • Amsterdam → Carolina via R33 — ~100 km, 1h 15 min — the scenic highland run, entirely on tar. Avoids the N2 Ermelo industrial route and keeps you in proper countryside. Look out for the dolerite rock formations as you approach Carolina.
  • Carolina → Dullstroom via R540 — ~90 km, 1 hr — fuel stop in Carolina if needed, then the final climb into the highlands. Dullstroom sits at 2,100m — expect cold, clear air and possibly frost on the dam edges.

Where to stay (2 farm-stay options for 6)

Blue Crane Farm (top pick — boys will love it)

Three 6-sleeper units (Otters Cove, Misty Waters, Weavers Nest) just 50m from the water's edge of a 9-hectare trout dam. Multiple fishing jetties, beginner-friendly fly fishing, full self-catering with fireplaces. The boys can fish off the jetty while you cook supper. ~R4,200/night for 6.

Millstream Farm

3 km from Dullstroom village. Spacious cottages sleeping up to 10, each with full kitchen, lounge with fireplace, private patio with braai overlooking the trout dams. From ~R2,820/night. South Africa's premier fly-fishing destination.

  • Anvil Ale brewery in Dullstroom (kid-safe garden, parents get a beer)
  • Walk the dam wall, build a fire by 4pm — dark by 5:30pm in June

Day 5 Tuesday 23 June

Dullstroom → Hoedspruit via Panorama Route [~230 km]

~230 km ~3.5h driving + 2–3h stops The grand finale

The grand finale

Modest distance, big day of sightseeing. Leave Dullstroom at 7am, climb Long Tom Pass, drop into Sabie for an early lunch, then the Panorama Route's greatest hits: God's Window, Bourke's Luck Potholes, the Three Rondavels viewpoint over the Blyde River Canyon. Drop down through the Strijdom Tunnel into the Lowveld and into Hoedspruit by early evening.

📍 Open Day 5 Panorama Route in Google Maps

Stops, in order

1. Long Tom Pass (Lydenburg → Sabie) — ~1 hr

The R37 climbs over 2,150m past the Long Tom cannon replica at the Devil's Knuckles. This pass is named after the final conventional battle of the Anglo-Boer War (8–11 September 1900). After the Battle of Bergendal, General Louis Botha retreated here with two Boer Long Tom siege guns — French-made 155mm Creusot cannons that could hurl a 38kg shell 10km. The British gave the guns their nickname. Botha successfully dragged both cannons up the near-vertical slope of Mauchsberg before the British could capture them — a remarkable feat. The bronze replica cannon at Devil's Knuckles marks the spot. Stop here — it's a 5-minute walk from the car park and the views down the escarpment are extraordinary.

2. Sabie — early lunch (11am-ish)

The Wild Fig Tree or Woodsman are reliable. Quick stop, then push on.

3. Mac Mac Falls — 20 min

Roadside viewpoint, no hiking needed. 65m double waterfall.

4. God's Window — 30 min

700m sheer drop over the Lowveld escarpment. Short walk from car park. Spectacular if it's not misted in (June is generally clear).

5. Bourke's Luck Potholes — 1 hr

Cylindrical river-carved potholes where the Blyde meets the Treur. Bridge walks, picnic lawns. R55/adult entry, payable on arrival.

6. Three Rondavels viewpoint — 30 min

The big one. View over Blyde River Canyon (third-largest canyon in the world). Stop at the lookout, take the photos, get back in the car.

7. Strijdom Tunnel → Hoedspruit — 1 hr

Drop ~1,000m down the escarpment through the tunnel. Look for baboons. Arrive Hoedspruit ~5–6pm.

Tactical timing:

  • Hard rule: be on the road by 7am from Dullstroom
  • If running late, the easiest cuts are Mac Mac Falls and God's Window — keep Bourke's Luck and Three Rondavels
  • Sunset in June is ~5:30pm. Don't try to do the Panorama viewpoints in dusk

Practical notes

Pack for cold (this is the big one)

June nights inland in SA are properly cold — Clarens, Dullstroom, Wakkerstroom all routinely drop below 0°C overnight. Daytime is gorgeous (15–22°C, sunny) but the moment the sun drops, it bites.

  • Beanies, gloves, thermal layers for everyone (the boys will need them at Bourke's Luck and Three Rondavels)
  • Hot water bottles or a thermos for the car
  • Down jacket or warm fleece each — fires take a while to warm a stone cottage
  • Closed shoes for farm mud at Funny Stone and the cliff viewpoints on Day 5

Self-catering food strategy

  • Big shop in Grahamstown on Day 1 before leaving — stock up on breakfast (rusks, fruit, yoghurt), snacks, lunch supplies, dinners for Funny Stone
  • Top-up shop in Aliwal North on Day 2 — restock for Clarens and beyond (good Total/Engen with food)
  • Coffee/tea, sugar, salt, oil — most farm stays don't supply pantry basics, double-check at booking
  • Boys love road-trip snack boxes pre-packed (jelly babies, biltong, naartjies)

Fuel strategy

  • Fill in Grahamstown at car pickup (Day 1)
  • Day 1: Queenstown is the main fuel stop en route to Barkly East
  • Day 2: Aliwal North (good Total/Engen with toilets and food — crosses into Free State here)
  • Day 3: Volksrust (bigger town with decent shops — lunch & fuel)
  • Day 4: Carolina if needed, then top up in Dullstroom on arrival
  • Day 5: Top up in Lydenburg before Long Tom Pass, then Sabie if needed

Car prep

  • Twin boys + two adults in the back: agree on a screen-time / book / nap schedule before Day 2 (the long one — 7.5h)
  • USB chargers + power inverter — long days will drain devices
  • Snack tub between the front seats so kids don't have to ask
  • Good road playlist — Spotify offline downloads (parts of the Free State have no signal)

Booking ASAP: June is mid-winter so it's quieter than summer holidays, but Clarens books up for school-holiday weekends and Dullstroom is busy with anglers year-round. Lock in stays at least 4–6 weeks ahead.

🧒 For the Boys — highlights along the whole route

Everything along the way that will actually excite the kids. Ordered by day.

Night 0 — Funny Stone, Barkly East (Saturday 19 June)

  • Naude's Nek — if the boys are awake and weather is clear, this pass (highest in SA at 2,874m) is worth a late-afternoon drive to the summit. Snow is possible in June. The rocks, the hairpins, and the sheer scale are genuinely impressive to kids. Check conditions →
  • Rhodes village trout fishing — 40 min from Funny Stone. The village has a dedicated kids' fishing spot on the Sterkstroom River. Rods for hire, no licence needed for juniors. Catch-and-release rainbow trout. rhodesvillage.co.za →
  • Ben McDhui Ski Resort — 45 min from Barkly East. If there's been early snow (common by mid-June), the ski tow is running and the snow sledding is brilliant for kids. Even without snow, the summit plateau at 3,000m is dramatic. benmcdhui.co.za →

Day 2 — En route to Clarens (Sunday 20 June)

  • Aliwal North Hot Springs — the overnight stop after Day 1 (if you break the journey) has a famous thermal springs resort. The pool water is 34°C even in winter — kids love it. Worth knowing about even if just driving past. Always Alival →
  • Fouriesburg — Surrender Hill viewpoint — from the R26 approaching Clarens, a short detour shows the hill where 4,300 Boers laid down arms. Hard to explain to adults, impossible to explain to kids — but the giant open landscape where all those men gave up is something they'll remember.
  • Clarens ice cream & Spruit Walk — the main street in Clarens is extremely walkable, with artisan ice cream (Clarens Creamery), quirky curio shops, and the Spruit Walk along the stream that kids can splash around in. The sandstone cliffs surrounding town glow orange in afternoon light. Let them run.
  • Highlight: the landscape itself — from Zastron through Fouriesburg to Clarens, the Maloti Mountains on the left are Lesotho. The road crosses open Free State farmland with nothing between you and the mountains but grass and sky. Dramatically different from the Eastern Cape.

Day 3 — Clarens → Wakkerstroom via Golden Gate (Monday ... wait, Sunday 21 June)

  • Golden Gate Highlands NP — Blesbok Loop — a 6.7km self-drive game circuit inside the park. You'll see zebra, blesbok, black wildebeest, and eland at close range, often from the car window. No hiking required. Open from sunrise. SANParks activities →
  • Cathedral Cave picnic — inside Golden Gate, a short walk (20 min return) leads to Cathedral Cave, a giant sandstone overhang with San bushman paintings. The cave is big enough that you could shelter a hundred people. Kids tend to go quiet in there.
  • Lichens Pass summit — 2,041m — on the R712 after Golden Gate you climb over a high pass into KwaZulu-Natal. Stop at the top: the views back toward the Drakensberg escarpment are huge, and if it's cold enough, the frost on the grass hasn't melted by 9am. Worth 5 minutes.
  • Volksrust — old Anglo-Boer War border post — the town sits on the old Natal/Transvaal border. In 1900 this was a forward British position. The name means "People's Rest" — where Boer families stopped fleeing the British. Quick fuel stop and a look at the Boer War memorial on the main street.

Day 4 — Wakkerstroom → Dullstroom (Monday 22 June)

  • Victorian walk, Wakkerstroom — before you leave, 20 minutes wandering the main street shows the boys what a Boer War–era town actually looked like. The General Store, the old church, and the stone houses around the green are all intact. There's a plaque marking where Boer commando snipers pinned down the British garrison.
  • Blue Crane Farm — trout dam jetties — the main event of Day 4. Multiple jetties on a 9-hectare dam. Rods for hire from the farm. The trout are stocked and aggressive — kids will catch fish. Beginner fly-fishing lessons available. A 9-hectare lake means they can also just throw sticks in. bluecranefarm.co.za →
  • Anvil Ale Brewery, Dullstroom — kid-safe garden with outdoor seating, lawn games, and a braai area. Parents get a craft beer, boys get juice and somewhere to run. Exactly what you need after a long day in the car.

Day 5 — Dullstroom → Hoedspruit via Panorama Route (Tuesday 23 June)

  • Long Tom Pass — bronze cannon replica — 5-min walk from the car park at Devil's Knuckles. A life-size replica of the French Creusot siege gun the Boers dragged up this pass in 1900. You can climb on it, stand next to it, and see why the British called it the Long Tom. The escarpment views from here are spectacular. Pass history →
  • Mac Mac Falls — 65m double waterfall, accessible from a roadside viewpoint in 2 minutes. No hike needed. The thunder of the water is genuinely impressive and the mist rises 20m. Named after Scottish miners who all had "Mac" in their surnames.
  • Bourke's Luck Potholes — the geological highlight of the day. The Blyde and Treur rivers carved perfectly cylindrical potholes into the rock over millions of years — some are 6m deep and 3m wide. There are suspension bridge walkways over the gorge. Kids go absolutely feral here. R55/adult entry. Allow 1 hour. Info →
  • Three Rondavels — Blyde River Canyon viewpoint — the "big view" of the whole trip. Three massive dolerite towers rising from the canyon floor, 800m below. The scale is genuinely shocking even for adults. Kids will spend 5 minutes staring and then want to climb the fence — hold onto them. The canyon is the third-largest in the world.
  • Descending the Strijdom Tunnel — the road drops 1,000m from the escarpment to the Lowveld in 30 minutes. Suddenly the vegetation changes from highland grass to subtropical bushveld, the temperature rises 10°C, and you start seeing roadside baboons. The change is instant and dramatic.

🎮 Games to play in the car

South Africa specials 🇿🇦

These ones are tailored to what's actually outside the window on this route.

SA Number Plate Province Hunt

South African plates carry a province code — first to spot all nine provinces wins. You'll see plenty on this route. Keep a tally on a notepad.

  • EC — Eastern Cape  •  WC — Western Cape  •  GP — Gauteng  •  KZN — KwaZulu-Natal
  • MP — Mpumalanga  •  LP — Limpopo  •  NW — North West  •  NC — Northern Cape  •  FS — Free State

Tip: GP plates are everywhere. LP and NC are the hard ones. You'll drive through MP, FS, EC and KZN borders on this trip — those should be easy.

Animal Bingo — SA Highveld Edition

Pick a target list before Day 2 (the Free State stretch has the most wildlife). First to spot all ten wins. Suggested targets:

  • Guinea fowl crossing the road (practically guaranteed — Day 2)
  • Hadeda ibis (noisy, unmistakeable)
  • Secretary bird in a field
  • Cattle egrets on a cow's back
  • Springbok  •  Blesbok  •  Zebra (Golden Gate — Day 3)
  • Baboon on the road (near the Strijdom Tunnel — Day 5)
  • Black crow on a fence post
  • A truck with livestock in the back (very common, Free State)

Boer War Quiz

After each day's drive, the boys (or anyone) gets quizzed on what they saw and heard. Use this itinerary as the answer sheet. Gets more competitive as the trip goes on.

  • What did the Boers call their guerrilla fighters? (Commandos)
  • What was the Long Tom? (French Creusot 155mm siege cannon)
  • What happened at Surrender Hill near Fouriesburg? (4,300 Boers laid down arms — largest single surrender of the war)
  • Who was the Boer general who escaped through Golden Gate? (Christiaan de Wet)
  • What does Wakkerstroom mean? ("Wakker" = alert/awake, "stroom" = stream — "Alert Stream")
  • What's a kopje? (Small rocky hill — from Dutch "little head")

Classics (upgraded)

Story Relay with a Twist

One person starts with a single sentence. Each player adds the next sentence. Every third turn, someone shouts a random word (banana, dragon, toenail) that must be worked into the story. Every five minutes: mandatory genre change. Horror story → romance → cooking show → spy thriller. The story will be unhinged by Carolina.

The Specific Spotter

Forget regular I Spy. Targets must be weirdly specific: a red truck with a dent, a dog in a bakkie, a billboard with someone pointing, a farm gate with a name that's also a person's name. First to ten wins. Harder than it sounds. Print a bingo card before you leave for max engagement.

Would You Rather: Survival Edition

Ridiculous survival dilemmas, but you must defend your answer for 30 seconds while everyone pokes holes in your logic. "Would you rather fight 100 duck-sized horses or one horse-sized duck?" "Would you rather be stranded in the Karoo in summer or on Naude's Nek in winter?" Keep it SA-relevant after Day 1.

Two Truths and a Whopper

Three "facts" — two true, one invented. Everyone votes on the lie. Ten-year-olds are shockingly good at this because they're genuinely sneaky and they learn weird stuff about their parents. Giles's facts about the Boer War are a natural source of material by Day 3.

Fortunately / Unfortunately

Person 1: "Fortunately, we found a treasure chest by the side of the R26." Person 2: "Unfortunately, it was guarded by a giant hadeda." Person 3: "Fortunately, the hadeda was afraid of rusks." Keep alternating. Descends into chaos fast. Perfect for the 11am doldrums of a long day.

The Movie Pitch

Someone names a three-word combo ("haunted farm auction", "talking springbok detective", "submarine braai competition"). Each person has 60 seconds to pitch it as a real movie — plot, cast, tagline. Everyone votes on the best pitch. Works best after Bourke's Luck when everyone's slightly delirious.

Geography Chain

Name a place. Next person names a place starting with the last letter of yours. New York → Kenya → Amsterdam → Mozambique → Ecuador. No repeats. Miss your turn, you're out. The boys will discover how many places end in A within two rounds.

Counting Cows (SA rules)

Each person picks a side of the road and counts cattle. The Free State stretch (Day 2) is prime territory. Rules:

  • Pass a cemetery on your side → lose all your cows
  • Pass a white horse → double your count
  • Pass a petrol station → +50
  • Pass a police car → everyone loses half (SA rules)
  • Pass a Spur restaurant → automatic win (rare after Aliwal North)

Alphabet Categories

Pick a category (SA animals, SA foods, Springboks players, African countries, Pokémon). Go A to Z around the car. Miss your letter, you're out. Last one standing wins. "African countries" will end careers around Q and X. That's the point.

Radio Roulette (works where there's signal — mostly Day 1 and Day 5)

Scan to a random station. Whatever's playing: guess the artist in 10 seconds, guess the year, or (if it's talk radio) predict what the host says next. Points for accuracy. Jacaranda FM will gift you mid-2000s hits on the Mpumalanga stretch.
