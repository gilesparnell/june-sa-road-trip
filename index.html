      Trip overview

      A 6-day road trip from the Eastern Cape coast up to Hoedspruit Airport, with two nights at Funny Stone for proper family time and a deliberate rest day before pushing north. Same total distance (~1,510 km), same showcase landscapes — coastal Eastern Cape, mistbelt Berg foothills, Free State sandstone, Mpumalanga highlands, Panorama Route — but the Panorama is spread over a slower Tuesday afternoon plus Wednesday morning, with the airport drive baked into the final morning. No Wakkerstroom; no Hoedspruit overnight. Last bed of the trip is in Graskop at the head of the Panorama Route.

      A note on driving times: All times shown are Google Maps drive time plus 20% to account for toilet stops, fuel, and snack breaks — essential with kids on board. Plan on a comfort stop roughly every 90 minutes.

      Four things to know going in:

      • Day 1 is the long day (Kenton → Funny Stone, 8h) — leave by 7am. Day 2 is the deliberate rest day. Day 3 (Funny Stone → Clarens via Maloti) is 5h. Day 4 (Clarens → Dullstroom) is the new longer day — 5.5h via Bethlehem and Standerton, since we're cutting out the Wakkerstroom stop. Day 5 is the easiest day, ~2.5h drive plus a Panorama-west afternoon loop.
      • Day 6 has a fixed deadline. Giles must be at Hoedspruit Airport by ~11:30am Wed for the Arathusa shuttle. That means leaving Graskop by 7am to fit Bourke's Luck Potholes and the Three Rondavels viewpoint on the way down. If running tight, those are the two stops to keep — the rest (Pinnacle, God's Window, Wonder View) is done Tuesday afternoon from Graskop.
      • June nights inland are COLD (often below freezing in the Berg, Free State, and Mpumalanga highlands). Every recommended stay has a working fireplace — bring beanies, gloves, and a thermos.
      • Self-catering throughout means you can leave at dawn without waiting for breakfast. Pre-pack travel mugs, fruit, and rusks for the road.

      Day 1 Friday 19 June

      Kenton-on-Sea → Barkly East (Funny Stone) [~468 km]

      ~468 km 8 hours driving ⚠ Leave Kenton by 7am

      The drive

      Leave Kenton by 7am — this is an 8h day. Pick up the hire car in Grahamstown, then head north-west on the R67 to Fort Beaufort, north through Adelaide to Queenstown, then south-east through Sterkstroom and Dordrecht as the road climbs steadily into the Berg foothills. Barkly East sits at 1,700m in a spectacular highland valley. Leaving at 7am puts you there around 3pm.

      What you'll see along the way

      • Ecca Pass (15 km north of Grahamstown) — tarred, built by road pioneer Andrew Geddes Bain in the 1800s. Beautiful geological formations — the Ecca shales are 250 million years old. A gentle warm-up pass before the day gets going.
      • Grahamstown (Makhanda) — the 1820 British Settlers landed on this frontier. The Battle of Grahamstown (1819) — where 10,000 Xhosa warriors attacked a British garrison of fewer than 400 — defined the colonial frontier. The whole Eastern Cape was contested territory for almost a century (nine Cape Frontier Wars, 1779–1878).
      • Fort Beaufort — founded in 1822 as a British military frontier post; the British army was here until 1870. The Martello Tower (a national monument — one of only two in the world with a full-swivel 360° cannon) is visible from the road. The War of the Axe (7th Frontier War, 1846) started here — sparked by the rescue of a prisoner being transferred to Grahamstown. The R67 you're driving was literally built by the British military as a strategic road from Grahamstown headquarters to the fort. They called it The Queen's Road.
      • Nico Malan Pass (Fort Beaufort → Whittlesea) — ranked in the top 25 altitude-gaining passes in South Africa. 673 vertical metres over 13.8 km — forested sections, steep cuttings, sweeping views, nicely banked corners. Road-trip forums consistently call this a joy to drive. Upgraded 2016.
      ⚠️ Do NOT take the Bedford road (R350). The road between Grahamstown and Bedford is chronically in poor condition — widely reported as undrivable. Take the R67 to Fort Beaufort instead. It was fully resurfaced in a R226 million SANRAL project (completed 2020): 70 km of new tar, widened shoulders. Adds ~30 min vs Bedford but is a completely different driving experience.
      📍 Open Day 1 route in Google Maps

      Route waypoints — take Fort Beaufort, not Bedford

      • Kenton-on-Sea → Grahamstown (Makhanda) — ~80 km, 1 hr — pick up hire car, fill the tank, big shop for Funny Stone days
      • Grahamstown → Fort Beaufort — 70 km, 1 hr — R67 (The Queen's Road) only. Do not take the Bedford road. The R67 is well-maintained resurfaced tar through the Ecca Pass (Ecca shales, 250 million years old) into frontier fort country
      • Fort Beaufort → Queenstown (Komani) — 140 km, 1.75 hrs — crosses Nico Malan Pass (673m climb over 13.8 km — one of SA's top 25 altitude-gaining passes). Good fuel & coffee stop in Queenstown before heading into the highlands
      • Queenstown → Dordrecht — 100 km, 1.25 hrs — road begins to climb into the Berg foothills; landscape changes noticeably
      • Dordrecht → Barkly East — 60 km, 1 hr — increasingly spectacular as you enter the highlands at 1,700m

      Funny Stone (family)

      Family property in Barkly East. Arrive Friday, unpack, light a fire, sleep deeply. Saturday is a deliberate rest day — see Day 2 for excursion options. You're here for two nights before pushing on to Clarens Sunday morning.

      If you arrive with energy

      • Walk the farm — the boys will want to explore before dark
      • Barkly East village is small but has a good butchery — grab boerewors for the braai
      • Sunset at altitude hits fast in June — fire on by 4:30pm

      Day 2 Saturday 20 June

      Rest day at Funny Stone [no driving]

      0 km on the road Family day Pick one excursion (or none)

      The point of today

      Day 1 is an 8-hour push and Day 3 is another 5-hour leg, so Saturday is deliberately empty — a buffer day. The most valuable thing it gives you is time with family at Funny Stone, which is the entire reason for the route's first half. Don't over-programme it. Late breakfast, walk the farm, kids loose in the veld, fire by mid-afternoon. The Funny Stone elders aren't getting any younger; the boys will remember a slow Saturday more than another viewpoint.

      That said — if you do want one outing, the Barkly East area is one of the most under-rated mountain destinations in South Africa, and you're already in it. Pick one, not all four. Save the rest for next time.

      Option A — Naude's Nek Pass (half-day, the iconic one)

      The highest road pass in South Africa at 2,587m — a gravel road over the Drakensberg, dropping into the Tina valley and Rhodes village on the far side. The full traverse to Maclear takes 4–5 hours each way; don't do that with kids. The right play is to drive to the summit, take photos, and turn back — a 3-hour round trip from Funny Stone. Snow is common in June; check road conditions the night before. 4x4 is not required but high-clearance tyres are smart. The hairpins, the cliff drops, and the sheer scale of the pass are genuinely awe-inspiring for ten-year-olds.

      📍 Funny Stone → Naude's Nek summit in Google Maps

      Option B — Rhodes village & trout fishing (half-day, kid-magnet)

      Rhodes is a tiny historic village ~40 minutes north of Barkly East, tucked at the base of the Drakensberg. Walkerbouts Inn rents kids' fishing rods; the Sterkstroom River has rainbow trout actively stocked for visiting anglers, and no licence is needed for juniors. There's a dedicated kids' fishing dam in the village. Wood-fired pizza or pancakes at Walkerbouts for lunch, then back to Funny Stone by 3pm. Lower-altitude than Naude's Nek so warmer; better in cold/wet weather when the pass might be a wash-out.

      📍 Funny Stone → Rhodes village in Google Maps

      Option C — Tiffindell / Ben McDhui (half-day, only if there's snow)

      South Africa's only ski resort, at 2,720m on the slopes of Ben McDhui (SA's second-highest peak). Operations are intermittent — check status before you commit. If the ski tow is running, this is a no-brainer for the boys: two-hour lift pass, a few runs, the novelty of skiing in Africa. If it isn't, you can still drive to the summit plateau (3,000m) and let the kids play in the snow patches. Conditions can be severe — closed shoes, layers, gloves are non-negotiable. The road is gravel but well-maintained when conditions allow.

      📍 Funny Stone → Tiffindell in Google Maps

      Option D — Barkly East railway zigzags (2 hours, the secret history)

      Just outside town: the disused Stormberg–Barkly East railway uses a series of eight reverses (zigzags) to climb the escarpment — one of only a handful of zigzag-reverse railway systems ever built. The track is still in place; you can park at the lower reverse and walk a section. The boys are old enough to grasp why engineers had to invent a way to climb a mountain a train couldn't simply ascend. Quick excursion with snacks and a flask. Closed shoes — rocky underfoot.

      Practical: Whatever you pick, be back at Funny Stone by 4pm at the latest. Fire by 4:30, dinner at 6, kids in bed by 8 — Day 3 (Funny Stone → Clarens) is a 5-hour drive starting at 7am Sunday morning, so Saturday night needs to end early.

      Day 3 Sunday 21 June

      Funny Stone → Clarens (Eastern Free State) [~360 km]

      ~360 km 5 hours driving ⚠ Leave Funny Stone by 7am

      The drive

      A long day but a rewarding one — the route change makes the difference. After Aliwal North, forget the flat inland road to Bethlehem: take the Maloti Route (Route 26) east, which follows the Lesotho border through the sandstone foothills. The Maluti Mountains are with you for 120 km — pink and orange cliffs with snow on the higher peaks in June. End with the spectacular R711 into Clarens. Leave Funny Stone by 7am and you'll arrive in Clarens around noon to 1pm.

      📍 Open Day 3 route in Google Maps (Maloti Route via Fouriesburg)

      Route waypoints (approximate — total ~360 km)

      • Barkly East → Lady Grey — ~65 km, 50 min — lovely foothills road through the Klein Berg. Lady Grey is named after the wife of British Governor Sir George Grey — the British colonial naming of the Eastern Cape is impossible to miss through here.
      • Lady Grey → Aliwal North — ~50 km, 45 min — crosses into the Free State at the Orange River. Fuel and food stop in Aliwal North. The town has Boer War blockhouse ruins and was the site of a British concentration camp during the Second Boer War.
      • Aliwal North → Zastron — ~85 km, 1 hr — now on the Maloti Route (Route 26), hugging the Lesotho border foothills. The Maluti Mountains begin to appear on your right — sandstone massifs that shift from pink to orange to gold as the light changes. This is the turn that makes Day 2 special.
      • Zastron → Fouriesburg (Route 26, Maloti Route) — ~120 km, 1.75 hrs — one of the most scenic drives in South Africa. The Maluti Mountains are a constant companion along the Lesotho border — snow-capped in June. Fouriesburg was the last capital of the Orange Free State during the Anglo-Boer War. After Bloemfontein fell to the British in March 1900, the Boer government relocated here. It didn't last: on 30 July 1900, General Marthinus Prinsloo surrendered 4,300 Boer troops at nearby Surrender Hill — the largest Boer capture of the entire war. You'll drive through this country.
      • Fouriesburg → Clarens (R711) — ~25 km, 20 min — save this last bit for the afternoon light. The R711 climbs through extraordinary rock formations, views over the Caledon River valley, snow-capped Maloti peaks on the horizon. This 25km stretch is consistently called one of the most beautiful short drives in South Africa.

      Historical note — Fouriesburg: Fouriesburg was the provisional capital of the Orange Free State Republic after Bloemfontein fell to Lord Roberts in March 1900. In the surrounding mountains, 4,300 Boer fighters were trapped and captured at "Surrender Hill" on 30 July 1900 — the biggest single capture of the entire Anglo-Boer War. Christiaan de Wet, who escaped through the Golden Gate with a small force the night before, called the surrender "a horrible murder of government, country and people." The town you drive through carries all of this history quietly.

      The Barbet Country Cottages (top pick)

      Partly thatched 3-bedroom cottage on a large farm 17 km from Clarens along the scenic Maluti route. Cosy sunroom with fireplace, fully equipped kitchen, sleeps 6+1. Big farm feel, space for the boys to run. ~R655 pp/night (≈ R3,930/night for 6). Exactly the vibe — thatched, fires, farm.

      Schaapplaats Farm Cottages

      Budget-friendly self-catering on a working farm between Fouriesburg and Clarens. Genuine farm setting, mountain views, fireplaces. Listed as one of the best budget farm stays in the area.

      budget-getaways.co.za — Schaapplaats →

      If you arrive with energy

      • Spruit Walk (Lake Clarens) — easy 30–45 min family trail, perfect leg-stretcher after a long day in the car
      • Clarens Brewery for the adults, ice cream at the square for the boys
      • Sunset on the koppies behind the village — sandstone glows golden in the last light

      Day 4 Monday 22 June

      Clarens → Dullstroom (via Bethlehem & Standerton) [~430 km]

      ~430 km 5.5 hours driving ⚠ Leave Clarens by 8am

      The drive

      The new longer day. Because we're skipping Wakkerstroom, this is the leg that absorbs the extra distance: Clarens out via Bethlehem, north through Reitz and Frankfort, across the highveld to Standerton, then east on the R23 / R36 through Carolina and up into Dullstroom. It's not the most dramatic stretch of road on the trip — the Free State maize-and-mielie country is wide open, agricultural, and quiet — but it moves well, the surface is good throughout, and a Monday in mid-winter means minimal traffic. Leave Clarens by 8am, fuel-and-coffee in Bethlehem, lunch in Standerton, arrive Dullstroom mid-afternoon. Full evening to settle in and a clear morning for fishing or a slow start on Day 5.

      If you want a slightly more scenic alternative, the route via Memel, Vrede and Volksrust adds ~30 km and is rolling rather than flat — same overall time. Both are mapped below.

      📍 Open Day 4 route in Google Maps (via Bethlehem & Standerton)

      Route waypoints (approximate — total ~430 km)

      • Clarens → Bethlehem — ~35 km, 35 min — easy warm-up on the R711/N5, leaving the sandstone country behind. Bethlehem is the first proper town: 24-hour fuel, Wimpy / Mugg & Bean for second breakfast, public toilets if you didn't quite catch them at Funny Stone. Stock up on water and snacks here — the next 200 km are mostly farm roads.
      • Bethlehem → Reitz → Frankfort — ~120 km, 1.5 hrs — north on the R26 through classic Free State maize country. Reitz was once Christiaan de Wet's home town and is still a quiet agricultural centre. The road is straight, the horizon is enormous, and the kids can play "spot the windpomp" (windmill water pumps) — there are hundreds.
      • Frankfort → Standerton — ~95 km, 1 hr — continues north on the R26 then east on the R23 across the Vaal River into Mpumalanga. The Vaal crossing is worth pointing out to the boys — it's the historic provincial boundary between the Free State and the old Transvaal. Standerton is a fuel-and-toilet stop, mid-trip lunch if anything decent is open (the Spur on the main road is reliable).
      • Standerton → Carolina (via R36 / R39) — ~110 km, 1.25 hrs — east through highland farmland. Carolina was a Boer War garrison town; the British built a chain of blockhouses through here in 1901 to try to choke the supply lines of de Wet's commandos. Quick fuel stop if needed.
      • Carolina → Dullstroom via R540 — ~75 km, 1 hr — the final climb into the highlands. Dullstroom sits at 2,100m — expect cold, clear air and possibly frost on the dam edges by the time you're parking the car.

      This is the day to keep moving. No big detours, no Panorama-style stops — Bethlehem coffee, Standerton lunch, Carolina fuel, then push to Dullstroom. The reward is a long peaceful afternoon and evening at a trout farm. Save the dawdling for Day 5.

      Where to stay (2 farm-stay options for 6)

      Blue Crane Farm (top pick — boys will love it)

      Three 6-sleeper units (Otters Cove, Misty Waters, Weavers Nest) just 50m from the water's edge of a 9-hectare trout dam. Multiple fishing jetties, beginner-friendly fly fishing, full self-catering with fireplaces. The boys can fish off the jetty while you cook supper. ~R4,200/night for 6.

      Millstream Farm

      3 km from Dullstroom village. Spacious cottages sleeping up to 10, each with full kitchen, lounge with fireplace, private patio with braai overlooking the trout dams. From ~R2,820/night. South Africa's premier fly-fishing destination.

      • Walk the dam wall, build a fire by 4pm — dark by 5:30pm in June

      Day 5 Tuesday 23 June

      Dullstroom → Graskop via Long Tom Pass [~145 km + Panorama afternoon loop]

      ~145 km drive 2.5h on the road + Panorama-west afternoon loop

      The easiest day, with a payoff

      Short driving, big rewards. Leave Dullstroom by 8am, climb Long Tom Pass with a proper cannon stop, drop into Sabie for an early lunch and the Mac Mac Falls roadside viewpoint, then visit Pilgrim's Rest — the perfectly preserved 1873 gold-rush village — before rolling into Graskop around 2pm. Check into the guest house, drop the bags, and head out late afternoon for the Panorama-west golden-hour loop — Pinnacle Rock, God's Window, and Wonder View, all within 25 minutes of Graskop and at their best in the 4–5pm light. Back to the guest house by 6pm for braai and an early night. The Wednesday-morning departure is non-negotiable, so this is the trip's last leisurely evening.

      📍 Open Day 5 morning route in Google Maps (Long Tom → Sabie → Mac Mac → Pilgrim's Rest → Graskop)

      Morning stops, in order

      1. Long Tom Pass — Devil's Knuckles cannon — ~45 min

      The R37 climbs over 2,150m past the Long Tom cannon replica at Devil's Knuckles. The pass is named after the final conventional battle of the Anglo-Boer War (8–11 September 1900). After the Battle of Bergendal, General Louis Botha retreated here with two Boer Long Tom siege guns — French-made 155mm Creusot cannons that could hurl a 38kg shell 10km. The British gave the guns their nickname. Botha successfully dragged both cannons up the near-vertical slope of Mauchsberg before the British could capture them — a remarkable feat. The bronze replica at Devil's Knuckles marks the spot; it's a 5-minute walk from the car park and the views down the escarpment are extraordinary. The boys can climb on it.

      2. Sabie — early lunch (~11:30am)

      The Wild Fig Tree or Woodsman are reliable family options. Sabie itself is a former forestry town and the heart of waterfall country — quiet, leafy, easy.

      3. Mac Mac Falls — 20 min

      Roadside viewpoint, no hiking needed. 65m double waterfall, the mist rising from the gorge is genuinely impressive. Named after Scottish miners who all had "Mac" in their surnames.

      4. Pilgrim's Rest — 1 to 1.5 hr

      The 1873 gold-rush village frozen in time. When alluvial gold was discovered in the Blyde River and its tributaries, hundreds of prospectors arrived overnight and threw up a tin-roofed town that ran for forty years before the deposits ran out. The state declared the entire village a national monument in 1986 and bought the original buildings; what you walk through is the actual town, not a reconstruction. The whole of the main street — Royal Hotel, Anglican church, jail, dredging-era stamp mill — is open to walk. The boys can pan for gold at the demonstration claim (the operators salt it so they always find something — they're nine, this is fine). 17 km west of Graskop on the R533, sitting in a forested valley that looks unchanged since the 1890s.

      📍 Pilgrim's Rest in Google Maps

      5. Graskop arrival — ~2pm

      17 km up the R533 from Pilgrim's Rest. Settle in, fuel the car for tomorrow, grab proper coffee or a stack at Harrie's Pancakes on the main street. Then back in the car around 3:30–4pm for the late-afternoon Panorama-west loop while the light's at its best.

      Afternoon — Panorama west loop from Graskop

      All three of these are along the R534/R532 north and west of Graskop, on a tight 60 km loop. Allow 3 hours including stops.

      📍 Open Panorama west loop in Google Maps

      Pinnacle Rock — 20 min

      A 30m sandstone tower rising from the forested escarpment. Easy 5-min walk to the viewpoint. Fewer crowds than God's Window — often the better photo stop.

      God's Window — 30 min

      700m sheer drop over the Lowveld escarpment. Short walk from the car park; the boys can do the slightly longer rainforest trail (15 min one-way) up to the cliff edge. Spectacular if it's not misted in (June is generally clear and dry — the dry season).

      Wonder View — 15 min

      Just up the road from God's Window — actually higher (1,730m) and arguably the better view. A quick photo stop on the way back to Graskop.

      Why do this in the afternoon, not Wednesday morning: Wednesday has a fixed airport deadline — you cannot afford to be queueing behind tour buses at God's Window with a flight on the clock. Doing the western viewpoints today means Wednesday only has the two big eastern stops (Bourke's Luck + Three Rondavels) on the way south, both en route, no detours.

      Where to stay — Graskop

      Graskop Hotel (centre of town, art-led, family rooms)

      Long-running hotel on the main street with an art collection, decent restaurant, family rooms that sleep 4–6. Walkable to dinner; secure parking. Reliable rather than spectacular — but secure, central, and the staff will brief you on the next morning's Panorama timing.

      graskophotel.co.za →

      Misty Mountain Reserve / Summit Lodge (escarpment-edge, view over Lowveld)

      Self-catering cabins on the escarpment ~10 min south of Graskop, with views straight off the cliff into the Lowveld. The chairlift down to the lower viewpoints is a kid favourite. Slightly less convenient for the Wednesday-morning route but visually unforgettable.

      mistymountain.co.za →

      Day 6 Wednesday 24 June

      Graskop → Hoedspruit Airport via Bourke's Luck & Three Rondavels [~145 km]

      ~145 km ~4 hours incl. two stops ⚠ Airport drop by 11:30am

      The deadline morning

      Leave Graskop by 7am. Two stops only — Bourke's Luck Potholes (opens 7am) and the Three Rondavels viewpoint — and you'll be at Hoedspruit Airport with comfortable time to spare. Giles steps onto the Arathusa shuttle; Jackie and the boys keep the car and drive on to Lissataba (45 min from the airport). The Panorama-west viewpoints (Pinnacle, God's Window, Wonder View) were knocked off Tuesday afternoon, so this morning is genuinely the two-stop run, not a sightseeing marathon against the clock.

      📍 Open Day 6 route in Google Maps

      Schedule (target arrival 11:00–11:30am)

      06:30 — Load the car & clear the room

      Last fuel check (should be full from last night). Kids in the car with rusks, juice, a blanket — they can sleep through the first 30 minutes.

      07:00 — Leave Graskop

      North on the R532. The drive to Bourke's Luck Potholes is ~35 km, ~40 min. The road is well-maintained tar through forested escarpment country.

      07:45 — Bourke's Luck Potholes (gate opens 07:00) — 45 min on site

      The geological highlight of the whole Panorama Route. The Blyde and Treur rivers carved perfectly cylindrical potholes into the rock over millions of years — some are 6m deep and 3m wide. There are suspension bridge walkways over the gorge. Kids go absolutely feral here. R55/adult entry, payable on arrival. Allow 45 min including the walk to the upper viewpoint and back. Leave by 08:45.

      09:00 — Three Rondavels Viewpoint — 20 min

      ~15 km north on the R532. Three massive dolerite towers rising from the floor of the Blyde River Canyon, 800m below — the third-largest canyon in the world. Stop at the lookout, take the photos, get back in the car. Worth the small detour up to the upper parking — the angle is markedly better. Leave by 09:30.

      09:30 — Descend via Strijdom Tunnel to Hoedspruit — ~1.5 hrs with kids

      Drop ~1,000m off the escarpment through the Strijdom Tunnel. The vegetation changes from highland grass to subtropical bushveld, the temperature rises 10°C, and you start seeing roadside baboons. Continue to Hoedspruit via the R36 / R531. Final 50 km is open Lowveld — fast, hot, and the change of scenery wakes everyone up.

      11:00–11:30 — Hoedspruit Airport drop-off

      Giles steps onto the Arathusa shuttle (departs the airport at 12:00 — confirm with the lodge the day before). Jackie + boys keep the rental car, refill the snack tub, and drive on to Lissataba (45 min).

      If you're running late at 09:00 (still at Bourke's Luck): skip Three Rondavels. The drive to the airport is the non-negotiable. Three Rondavels is photogenic but adds ~45 min including the detour up to the viewpoint. Bourke's Luck is the more memorable stop for the boys.

      If you're running early at 09:00 (already past Three Rondavels): there's a Hoedspruit Reptile Park / Endangered Species Centre on the airport road if you have an hour to kill — but the airport itself has a decent café and the boys will be ready to settle. Don't add stops just because you have time. Use it.

      Practical notes

      Pack for cold (this is the big one)

      June nights inland in SA are properly cold — Barkly East, Clarens, Dullstroom and Graskop all routinely drop below 0°C overnight. Daytime is gorgeous (15–22°C, sunny) but the moment the sun drops, it bites.

      • Beanies, gloves, thermal layers for everyone (the boys will need them at Bourke's Luck and Three Rondavels on Day 6)
      • Hot water bottles or a thermos for the car
      • Down jacket or warm fleece each — fires take a while to warm a stone cottage
      • Closed shoes for farm mud at Funny Stone, the Saturday excursion (Naude's Nek / Rhodes), and the Panorama viewpoints

      Self-catering food strategy

      • Big shop in Grahamstown on Day 1 before leaving — stock up on breakfast (rusks, fruit, yoghurt), snacks, lunch supplies, dinners for two nights at Funny Stone
      • Top-up shop in Bethlehem on Day 4 — restock for Dullstroom and Graskop (Pick n Pay / Spar on the main road)
      • Coffee/tea, sugar, salt, oil — most farm stays don't supply pantry basics, double-check at booking
      • Boys love road-trip snack boxes pre-packed (jelly babies, biltong, naartjies)

      Fuel strategy

      • Fill in Grahamstown at car pickup (Day 1)
      • Day 1: Queenstown is the main fuel stop en route to Barkly East
      • Day 3: Aliwal North (good Total/Engen with toilets and food — crosses into Free State here)
      • Day 4: Bethlehem at the start of the day for fuel + coffee; Standerton mid-trip if needed; Carolina before the final climb to Dullstroom
      • Day 5: Top up in Lydenburg before Long Tom Pass, then top up again in Graskop tonight (not tomorrow) so Wednesday morning starts on a full tank
      • Day 6: No fuel stops between Graskop and the airport — tank must be full at 7am

      Car prep

      • Twin boys + two adults in the back: agree on a screen-time / book / nap schedule before Day 1 (the long one — 8h)
      • USB chargers + power inverter — long days will drain devices
      • Snack tub between the front seats so kids don't have to ask
      • Good road playlist — Spotify offline downloads (parts of the Free State have no signal)

      Booking ASAP: June is mid-winter so it's quieter than summer holidays, but Clarens books up for school-holiday weekends and Dullstroom is busy with anglers year-round. Graskop is in peak season because of the Panorama traffic — book at least 6 weeks ahead.

      🧒 For the Boys — highlights along the whole route

      Everything along the way that will actually excite the kids. Ordered by day.

      Day 1 — Friday 19 June (Kenton → Funny Stone)

      • Ecca Pass shales — 250-million-year-old rock layers in the pass cuttings. Boys can see the age of the planet written in stripes. 15 km north of Grahamstown.
      • Fort Beaufort Martello Tower — visible from the R67, one of only two in the world with a full-swivel 360° cannon. Ten minutes off the road if you want to stop.
      • Nico Malan Pass — 673m climb in 13.8 km — top-25 altitude-gaining pass in SA. Kids tend to be glued to the window for the steep sections.

      Day 2 — Saturday 20 June (Rest day at Funny Stone)

      • Naude's Nek summit drive — the highest road pass in SA (2,587m). The hairpins, cliff drops, and possible snow are genuinely awe-inspiring. Do the summit and back, not the full traverse — 3-hour round trip from Funny Stone.
      • Rhodes village trout fishing — 40 min from Funny Stone. Dedicated kids' fishing spot on the Sterkstroom River, rods for hire, no licence needed for juniors. Catch-and-release rainbow trout. rhodesvillage.co.za →
      • Tiffindell / Ben McDhui — 45 min from Barkly East. If there's been early snow (common by mid-June), the ski tow is running and the snow sledding is brilliant for kids. Even without snow, the summit plateau at 3,000m is dramatic. benmcdhui.co.za →
      • Barkly East railway zigzag reverses — one of only a handful of zigzag-reverse railway systems ever built. Park at the lower reverse and walk a section. Quick excursion, surprisingly engaging for ten-year-olds once the engineering clicks.

      Day 3 — Sunday 21 June (Funny Stone → Clarens via Maloti Route)

      • Aliwal North Hot Springs — fuel-stop town has a famous thermal springs resort. The pool water is 34°C even in winter — kids love it. Worth knowing about even if just driving past. Always Alival →
      • Maluti Mountains, sandstone country — from Zastron through Fouriesburg to Clarens, the Maluti Mountains on the left are Lesotho. The road crosses open Free State farmland with nothing between you and the snow-capped peaks but grass and sky. Dramatically different from the Eastern Cape.
      • Surrender Hill — Fouriesburg — a short detour shows the hill where 4,300 Boers laid down arms. Hard to explain to adults, impossible to explain to kids — but the giant open landscape where all those men gave up is something they'll remember.
      • Clarens ice cream & Spruit Walk — the main street in Clarens is extremely walkable, with artisan ice cream (Clarens Creamery), quirky curio shops, and the Spruit Walk along the stream. Sandstone cliffs glow orange in afternoon light. Let them run.

      Day 4 — Monday 22 June (Clarens → Dullstroom)

      • Vaal River crossing — the boundary between the old Free State and the old Transvaal. A historic border worth pointing out from the bridge — easy "where in SA are we now" geography moment.
      • Windpomp counting through the Free State — endless wind-pump water pumps dotted across the maize country. Make it a game — 50 windpomps = R10 ice cream from the Spar in Bethlehem.
      • Blue Crane Farm — trout dam jetties — the main event of Day 4. Multiple jetties on a 9-hectare dam. Rods for hire from the farm; trout are stocked and aggressive — kids will catch fish. Beginner fly-fishing lessons available. bluecranefarm.co.za →
      • Anvil Ale Brewery, Dullstroom — kid-safe garden with outdoor seating, lawn games, and a braai area. Parents get a craft beer, boys get juice and somewhere to run. Exactly what you need after a long day in the car.

      Day 5 — Tuesday 23 June (Dullstroom → Graskop, Panorama afternoon)

      • Long Tom Pass — bronze cannon replica — 5-min walk from the car park at Devil's Knuckles. A life-size replica of the French Creusot siege gun the Boers dragged up this pass in 1900. You can climb on it, stand next to it, and see why the British called it the Long Tom. The escarpment views from here are spectacular. Pass history →
      • Mac Mac Falls — 65m double waterfall, accessible from a roadside viewpoint in 2 minutes. No hike needed. The thunder of the water is genuinely impressive and the mist rises 20m. Named after Scottish miners who all had "Mac" in their surnames.
      • Pilgrim's Rest — the gold-rush village — a perfectly preserved 1873 mining town, declared a national monument and frozen in place. The boys can pan for gold at the demonstration claim (the operators salt it — they always find something). Walk the main street: Royal Hotel, Anglican church, original jail, stamp mill. Unchanged since the 1890s. 17 km west of Graskop on the R533.
      • Pinnacle Rock — 30m sandstone tower rising from the forested escarpment. Easy 5-min walk to the viewpoint. Often less crowded than God's Window — better photo stop.
      • God's Window — 700m sheer drop over the Lowveld. The boys can do the short rainforest trail (15 min up) to the cliff edge. Holding on to them at the top is non-negotiable.
      • Graskop Hotel pancakes — Harrie's Pancakes on the main street is a Graskop institution. Order the savoury chicken-and-mushroom for dinner; the boys will be sticky with the sweet versions for pudding.

      Day 6 — Wednesday 24 June (Graskop → Hoedspruit Airport)

      • Bourke's Luck Potholes — suspension bridges — the geological highlight of the whole trip. Cylindrical river-carved potholes some 6m deep and 3m wide, with suspension-bridge walkways over the gorge. Kids go absolutely feral here. R55/adult entry. Plan 45 minutes. Info →
      • Three Rondavels — Blyde River Canyon viewpoint — the "big view" of the whole trip. Three massive dolerite towers rising from the canyon floor, 800m below. The scale is genuinely shocking even for adults. The canyon is the third-largest in the world.
      • Strijdom Tunnel descent — the road drops 1,000m off the escarpment in 30 minutes. Vegetation changes from highland grass to subtropical bushveld, the temperature rises 10°C, and you start seeing roadside baboons. The change is instant and dramatic. End-of-trip energy lifter for the back seat.

      🎮 Games to play in the car

      Six days (one of them a rest day), ~1,510km on the road, two ten-year-olds. Come prepared. These work without screens, signal, or any equipment.

      South Africa specials 🇿🇦

      These ones are tailored to what's actually outside the window on this route.

      SA Number Plate Province Hunt

      South African plates carry a province code — first to spot all nine provinces wins. You'll see plenty on this route. Keep a tally on a notepad.

      • EC — Eastern Cape  •  WC — Western Cape  •  GP — Gauteng  •  KZN — KwaZulu-Natal
      • MP — Mpumalanga  •  LP — Limpopo  •  NW — North West  •  NC — Northern Cape  •  FS — Free State

      Tip: GP plates are everywhere. LP and NC are the hard ones. You'll drive through MP, FS, EC and KZN borders on this trip — those should be easy.

      Animal Bingo — SA Highveld Edition

      Pick a target list before Day 2 (the Free State stretch has the most wildlife). First to spot all ten wins. Suggested targets:

      • Guinea fowl crossing the road (practically guaranteed — Day 2)
      • Hadeda ibis (noisy, unmistakeable)
      • Secretary bird in a field
      • Cattle egrets on a cow's back
      • Springbok  •  Blesbok  •  Zebra (Golden Gate — Day 3)
      • Baboon on the road (near the Strijdom Tunnel — Day 5)
      • Black crow on a fence post
      • A truck with livestock in the back (very common, Free State)

      Boer War Quiz

      After each day's drive, the boys (or anyone) gets quizzed on what they saw and heard. Use this itinerary as the answer sheet. Gets more competitive as the trip goes on.

      • What did the Boers call their guerrilla fighters? (Commandos)
      • What was the Long Tom? (French Creusot 155mm siege cannon)
      • What happened at Surrender Hill near Fouriesburg? (4,300 Boers laid down arms — largest single surrender of the war)
      • Who was the Boer general who escaped through Golden Gate? (Christiaan de Wet)
      • What does Wakkerstroom mean? ("Wakker" = alert/awake, "stroom" = stream — "Alert Stream")
      • What's a kopje? (Small rocky hill — from Dutch "little head")

      Classics (upgraded)

      Story Relay with a Twist

      One person starts with a single sentence. Each player adds the next sentence. Every third turn, someone shouts a random word (banana, dragon, toenail) that must be worked into the story. Every five minutes: mandatory genre change. Horror story → romance → cooking show → spy thriller. The story will be unhinged by Carolina.

      The Specific Spotter

      Forget regular I Spy. Targets must be weirdly specific: a red truck with a dent, a dog in a bakkie, a billboard with someone pointing, a farm gate with a name that's also a person's name. First to ten wins. Harder than it sounds. Print a bingo card before you leave for max engagement.

      Would You Rather: Survival Edition

      Ridiculous survival dilemmas, but you must defend your answer for 30 seconds while everyone pokes holes in your logic. "Would you rather fight 100 duck-sized horses or one horse-sized duck?" "Would you rather be stranded in the Karoo in summer or on Naude's Nek in winter?" Keep it SA-relevant after Day 1.

      Two Truths and a Whopper

      Three "facts" — two true, one invented. Everyone votes on the lie. Ten-year-olds are shockingly good at this because they're genuinely sneaky and they learn weird stuff about their parents. Giles's facts about the Boer War are a natural source of material by Day 3.

      Fortunately / Unfortunately

      Person 1: "Fortunately, we found a treasure chest by the side of the R26." Person 2: "Unfortunately, it was guarded by a giant hadeda." Person 3: "Fortunately, the hadeda was afraid of rusks." Keep alternating. Descends into chaos fast. Perfect for the 11am doldrums of a long day.

      The Movie Pitch

      Someone names a three-word combo ("haunted farm auction", "talking springbok detective", "submarine braai competition"). Each person has 60 seconds to pitch it as a real movie — plot, cast, tagline. Everyone votes on the best pitch. Works best after Bourke's Luck when everyone's slightly delirious.

      Geography Chain

      Name a place. Next person names a place starting with the last letter of yours. New York → Kenya → Amsterdam → Mozambique → Ecuador. No repeats. Miss your turn, you're out. The boys will discover how many places end in A within two rounds.

      Counting Cows (SA rules)

      Each person picks a side of the road and counts cattle. The Free State stretch (Day 2) is prime territory. Rules:

      • Pass a cemetery on your side → lose all your cows
      • Pass a white horse → double your count
      • Pass a petrol station → +50
      • Pass a police car → everyone loses half (SA rules)
      • Pass a Spur restaurant → automatic win (rare after Aliwal North)

      Alphabet Categories

      Pick a category (SA animals, SA foods, Springboks players, African countries, Pokémon). Go A to Z around the car. Miss your letter, you're out. Last one standing wins. "African countries" will end careers around Q and X. That's the point.

      Radio Roulette (works where there's signal — mostly Day 1 and Day 5)

      Scan to a random station. Whatever's playing: guess the artist in 10 seconds, guess the year, or (if it's talk radio) predict what the host says next. Points for accuracy. Jacaranda FM will gift you mid-2000s hits on the Mpumalanga stretch.
