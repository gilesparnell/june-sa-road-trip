Trip overview

This is a 4-day road trip from the Eastern Cape coast up to Hoedspruit, designed around rural farm stays, kid-friendly outdoor adventure, and avoiding Johannesburg entirely. The route hand-picks four very different landscapes: mistbelt forest, Free State sandstone country, the highveld trout meccas, and the Panorama Route's spectacular cliffs.

Three trade-offs to know going in:

  • Days 2 and 3 are 7–8 hours of driving — unavoidable given the distance and the no-Joburg constraint. Best mitigated with a 7am start, in-car movies, and snack supplies.
  • June nights inland are COLD (often below freezing in the Berg, Free State, and Mpumalanga highlands). Every recommended stay has a working fireplace — bring beanies, gloves, and a thermos.
  • Self-catering throughout means you can leave at dawn without waiting for breakfast. Pre-pack travel mugs, fruit, and rusks for the road.

Day 1 Saturday 20 June

Kenton-on-Sea → Hogsback

~280 km 3.5 hours driving Easy first day

The drive

Pick up the hire car in Grahamstown around 8am. Easy run via the N2 / R67 / R63 through Fort Beaufort and Alice, then the steep climb up to the Hogsback escarpment. Arrive by lunchtime with a full afternoon in the village.

Route waypoints

  • Kenton-on-Sea → Grahamstown (Makhanda) — 50 km, 45 min
  • Grahamstown → Fort Beaufort — 90 km, 1 hr
  • Fort Beaufort → Alice → Hogsback — 80 km, 1.5 hrs (final 30 km is winding mountain pass)

Where to stay (3 farm-stay options for 6)

Day 2 Sunday 21 June

Hogsback → Clarens (Eastern Free State)

~600 km 7–8 hours driving ⚠ Long day — 7am start

The drive

This is the toughest day. Aim to leave Hogsback by 7am — the road off the escarpment is best done in daylight. Coffee in Cathcart, fuel and bathroom break in Aliwal North (river crossing into Free State), lunch around Bethlehem. Arrive Clarens by 3–4pm.

Route waypoints

  • Hogsback → Cathcart — 90 km, 1.25 hrs
  • Cathcart → Queenstown (Komani) — 80 km, 1 hr (good fuel/coffee stop)
  • Queenstown → Aliwal North — 215 km, 2.5 hrs
  • Aliwal North → Zastron → Wepener → Excelsior → Bethlehem — 200 km, 2.25 hrs
  • Bethlehem → Clarens — 30 km, 25 min

Where to stay (3 farm-stay options for 6)

The Rose House on Schaapplaats Farm (top pick)

On a 580-hectare working farm between Fouriesburg and Clarens. The main sandstone house dates to 1923 and is built from local Free State sandstone. Genuine farm setting, mountain views, fireplaces. Just out of town so the boys have space to run wild.

Day 3 Monday 22 June

Clarens → Dullstroom

~620 km 7–8 hours driving ⚠ Long day — 7am start

The drive — the no-Joburg route

This is the day you skirt above Joburg. Route runs Clarens → Bethlehem → Vrede → Volksrust (KZN/Mpumalanga border) → Standerton → Ermelo → Carolina → Belfast → Dullstroom. All tar, mostly quiet roads through farmland. Leave by 7am, lunch in Volksrust or Standerton, arrive Dullstroom mid-afternoon.

Route waypoints

  • Clarens → Bethlehem → Vrede — 200 km, 2.5 hrs
  • Vrede → Volksrust — 175 km, 2 hrs (lunch & fuel)
  • Volksrust → Ermelo → Carolina — 200 km, 2.5 hrs
  • Carolina → Belfast → Dullstroom — 110 km, 1.25 hrs

Where to stay (3 farm-stay options for 6)

Blue Crane Farm (top pick — boys will love it)

Three 6-sleeper units (Otters Cove, Misty Waters, Weavers Nest) just 50m from the water's edge of a 9-hectare trout dam. Multiple fishing jetties, beginner-friendly fly fishing, full self-catering with fireplaces. The boys can fish off the jetty while you cook supper.

bluecranefarm.co.za →

Millstream Farm

3 km from Dullstroom village. Spacious double-storey cottages sleeping up to 10, each with full kitchen, lounge with fireplace, private patio with braai overlooking the trout dams. South Africa's premier fly-fishing destination.

millstreamfarm.co.za →

Schaefer's Halt

Historic country cottages on a working guest farm. Fishing, hiking, big skies. More rustic feel.

schaefershalt.com →

Afternoon for the boys

  • Beginner fly-fishing lesson on the dam — most farms hire out rods + offer a quick coaching session
  • Anvil Ale brewery in Dullstroom (kid-safe garden, parents get a beer)
  • Walk the dam wall, build a fire by 4pm — dark by 5:30pm in June
Day 4 Tuesday 23 June

Dullstroom → Hoedspruit via Panorama Route

~270 km 6–7 hours with sightseeing stops The grand finale

The grand finale

Modest distance, big day of sightseeing. Leave Dullstroom at 7am, climb Long Tom Pass, drop into Sabie for an early lunch, then the Panorama Route's greatest hits: God's Window, Bourke's Luck Potholes, the Three Rondavels viewpoint over the Blyde River Canyon. Drop down through the Strijdom Tunnel into the Lowveld and into Hoedspruit by early evening.

Stops, in order

1. Long Tom Pass (Lydenburg → Sabie) — ~1 hr

R37 climbs over 2,150m. Stop at the cannon replica viewpoint. Look for cyclists you can wave at.

2. Sabie — early lunch (11am-ish)

The Wild Fig Tree or Woodsman are reliable. Quick stop, then push on.

3. Mac Mac Falls — 20 min

Roadside viewpoint, no hiking needed. 65m double waterfall.

4. God's Window — 30 min

700m sheer drop over the Lowveld escarpment. Short walk from car park. Spectacular if it's not misted in (June is generally clear).

5. Bourke's Luck Potholes — 1 hr

Cylindrical river-carved potholes where the Blyde meets the Treur. Bridge walks, picnic lawns. R55/adult entry, payable on arrival.

6. Three Rondavels viewpoint — 30 min

The big one. View over Blyde River Canyon (third-largest canyon in the world). Stop at the lookout, take the photos, get back in the car.

7. Strijdom Tunnel → Hoedspruit — 1 hr

Drop ~1,000m down the escarpment through the tunnel. Look for baboons. Arrive Hoedspruit ~5–6pm.

Tactical timing:

  • Hard rule: be on the road by 7am from Dullstroom
  • If running late, the easiest cuts are Mac Mac Falls and God's Window — keep Bourke's Luck and Three Rondavels
  • Sunset in June is ~5:30pm. Don't try to do the Panorama viewpoints in dusk

Practical notes

Pack for cold (this is the big one)

June nights inland in SA are properly cold — Clarens, Dullstroom, Wakkerstroom all routinely drop below 0°C overnight. Daytime is gorgeous (15–22°C, sunny) but the moment the sun drops, it bites.

  • Beanies, gloves, thermal layers for everyone (the boys will need them at Bourke's Luck and Three Rondavels)
  • Hot water bottles or a thermos for the car
  • Down jacket or warm fleece each — fires take a while to warm a stone cottage
  • Closed shoes for Hogsback forest mud and the cliff viewpoints

Self-catering food strategy

  • Big shop in Grahamstown before leaving Day 1 — stock up on breakfast (rusks, fruit, yoghurt), snacks, lunch supplies, two dinners
  • Top-up shop in Clarens or Bethlehem on Day 2 evening for the rest
  • Coffee/tea, sugar, salt, oil — most farm stays don't supply pantry basics, double-check at booking
  • Boys love road-trip snack boxes pre-packed (jelly babies, biltong, naartjies)

Fuel strategy

  • Fill in Grahamstown at car pickup
  • Day 2: Aliwal North (good Total/Engen with toilets and food)
  • Day 3: Volksrust (bigger town, better stops than Vrede)
  • Day 4: Top up in Lydenburg before Long Tom Pass, then Sabie if needed

Car prep

  • Twin boys + two adults in the back: agree on a screen-time / book / nap schedule before Day 2
  • USB chargers + power inverter — long days will drain devices
  • Snack tub between the front seats so kids don't have to ask
  • Good road playlist — Spotify offline downloads (parts of the Free State have no signal)

Booking ASAP: June is mid-winter so it's quieter than summer holidays, but Clarens books up for school-holiday weekends and Dullstroom is busy with anglers year-round. Lock in stays at least 4–6 weeks ahead.
