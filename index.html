Trip overview

A 5-day road trip from the Eastern Cape coast up to Hoedspruit, designed around rural farm stays, kid-friendly outdoor adventure, and avoiding Johannesburg entirely. The route hand-picks five very different landscapes: coastal Eastern Cape, mistbelt Berg foothills, Free State sandstone country, the highveld birding villages, the Mpumalanga trout highlands, and the Panorama Route's spectacular cliffs.

A note on driving times: All times shown are Google Maps drive time plus 20% to account for toilet stops, fuel, and snack breaks — essential with kids on board. For example, Google Maps shows Day 2 (Barkly East → Clarens) as 5h20m; we're showing 6.5h. Plan on a comfort stop roughly every 90 minutes.

Three things to know going in:

  • Day 1 is the long day (Kenton → Barkly East, 8h) — leave by 7am. Day 2 is 6.5h (Barkly East → Clarens). Days 3 and 4 are 6.5h and 5h. Day 5 is ~3.5h driving broken up by Panorama Route stops.
  • June nights inland are COLD (often below freezing in the Berg, Free State, and Mpumalanga highlands). Every recommended stay has a working fireplace — bring beanies, gloves, and a thermos.
  • Self-catering throughout means you can leave at dawn without waiting for breakfast. Pre-pack travel mugs, fruit, and rusks for the road.

Day 1 Friday 19 June

Kenton-on-Sea → Barkly East (Funny Stone) [~468 km]

~468 km 8 hours driving ⚠ Leave Kenton by 7am

The drive

Leave Kenton by 7am — this is an 8h day. Pick up the hire car in Grahamstown, then head north-west on the R67 to Fort Beaufort, north through Adelaide to Queenstown, then south-east through Sterkstroom and Dordrecht as the road climbs steadily into the Berg foothills. Barkly East sits at 1,700m in a spectacular highland valley. Leaving at 7am puts you there around 3pm.

⚠️ Do NOT take the Bedford road (R350). The road between Grahamstown and Bedford is chronically in poor condition — widely reported as undrivable. Take the R67 to Fort Beaufort instead. It was fully resurfaced in a R226 million SANRAL project (completed 2020): 70 km of new tar, widened shoulders. Adds ~30 min vs Bedford but is a completely different driving experience.
📍 Open Day 1 route in Google Maps

Route waypoints — take Fort Beaufort, not Bedford

  • Kenton-on-Sea → Grahamstown (Makhanda) — ~80 km, 1 hr — pick up hire car, fill the tank, big shop for Funny Stone days
  • Grahamstown → Fort Beaufort — 70 km, 1 hr — R67 only. Do not take the Bedford road. The R67 is well-maintained resurfaced tar through the Ecca Pass
  • Fort Beaufort → Queenstown (Komani) — 140 km, 1.75 hrs — good fuel & coffee stop in Queenstown before heading into the highlands
  • Queenstown → Dordrecht — 100 km, 1.25 hrs — road begins to climb into the Berg foothills; landscape changes noticeably
  • Dordrecht → Barkly East — 60 km, 1 hr — increasingly spectacular as you enter the highlands at 1,700m

Where you're staying — Funny Stone

Funny Stone (family)

Family property in Barkly East. Arrive, unpack, light a fire. You've earned it — the rest of the trip is from here.

If you arrive with energy

  • Walk the farm — the boys will want to explore before dark
  • Barkly East village is small but has a good butchery — grab boerewors for the braai
  • Sunset at altitude hits fast in June — fire on by 4:30pm
Day 2 Saturday 20 June

Barkly East → Clarens (Eastern Free State) [~489 km]

~489 km 6.5 hours driving ⚠ Leave Funny Stone by 7am

The drive

The longest leg of the trip — tackle it fresh after a good night at Funny Stone. Head north-west through the Klein Drakensberg foothills via Lady Grey, cross the Orange River into the Free State at Aliwal North, then track north through sandstone Free State farmland to Clarens. 20 June is the winter solstice: clear, cold, beautiful. Leave by 7am and you'll roll into Clarens around 1:30–2pm.

📍 Open Day 2 route in Google Maps

Route waypoints (approximate — total ~489 km)

  • Barkly East → Lady Grey — ~60 km, 50 min — lovely foothills road through the Klein Berg
  • Lady Grey → Aliwal North — ~65 km, 55 min — crosses into the Free State at the Orange River; fuel stop here
  • Aliwal North → Zastron → Wepener — ~110 km, 1.5 hrs — flat Free State farmland
  • Wepener → Bethlehem — ~220 km, 2.5 hrs — lunch stop in Bethlehem
  • Bethlehem → Clarens — ~30 km, 25 min — sandstone cliffs appear as you approach

Where to stay (2 farm-stay options for 6)

The Barbet Country Cottages (top pick)

Partly thatched 3-bedroom cottage on a large farm 17 km from Clarens along the scenic Maluti route. Cosy sunroom with fireplace, fully equipped kitchen, sleeps 6+1. Big farm feel, space for the boys to run. ~R655 pp/night (≈ R3,930/night for 6). Exactly the vibe — thatched, fires, farm.

wheretostay.co.za — Barbet Cottages →

Schaapplaats Farm Cottages

Budget-friendly self-catering on a working farm between Fouriesburg and Clarens. Genuine farm setting, mountain views, fireplaces. Listed as one of the best budget farm stays in the area.

budget-getaways.co.za — Schaapplaats →

If you arrive with energy

  • Spruit Walk (Lake Clarens) — easy 30–45 min family trail, perfect leg-stretcher after a long day in the car
  • Clarens Brewery for the adults, ice cream at the square for the boys
  • Sunset on the koppies behind the village — sandstone glows golden in the last light
Day 3 Sunday 21 June

Clarens → Wakkerstroom (Mpumalanga) [~267 km]

~267 km 6.5 hours driving No rush — arrive late afternoon

The drive

A comfortable day after the long haul yesterday. Head north-east out of Clarens through Bethlehem and Vrede, cross into Mpumalanga and drop down to Volksrust, then wind through rolling highveld to Wakkerstroom. You're now firmly in Mpumalanga — the mood shifts from sandstone to wide-open grassland. Arrive by mid-afternoon, light the fire, and rest up before the final push to Dullstroom tomorrow.

📍 Open Day 3 route in Google Maps

Route waypoints (approximate — total ~267 km)

  • Clarens → Bethlehem → Vrede — ~130 km, 1.5 hrs — flat Free State farming roads, easy Sunday driving
  • Vrede → Volksrust — ~80 km, 1 hr — cross from Free State into Mpumalanga / KZN border; fuel and lunch stop in Volksrust
  • Volksrust → Wakkerstroom — ~55 km, 45 min — winding highveld road through open grassland

Where to stay

Wakkerstroom self-catering / guest house (book early — limited for 6)

Wakkerstroom is a tiny Victorian-era village — the accommodation pool is small but charming. Look for self-catering cottages that sleep 6 on Airbnb, SafariNow, or wakkerstroom.co.za. The village has a fireplace-and-braai culture; most properties are cosy and well set up for winter. Options include Ingwe Pan and Wakkerstroom Bush Camp.

wakkerstroom.co.za — accommodation →

Book early: Wakkerstroom is a very small town. Self-catering for 6 people is limited — don't leave this to the last minute.

Wakkerstroom highlights

  • Birding — Wakkerstroom is one of South Africa's top birding destinations. Bald Ibis, Blue Crane, and Wattled Crane are common. Even non-birders find it peaceful
  • Victorian village walk — stone buildings, original storefronts, a proper small-town feel. Easy 30-min wander with the kids
  • Evening fire — at 1,600m, June evenings in Wakkerstroom are properly cold and perfectly clear. Big braai, early night, ready for tomorrow's easy drive to Dullstroom
Day 4 Monday 22 June

Wakkerstroom → Dullstroom [~270 km]

~270 km 5 hours driving Easy day — arrive early afternoon

The drive

The easiest driving day of the trip. Head east through Ermelo and Carolina into the Mpumalanga highlands, climbing steadily as the landscape shifts from open grassland to misty highlands. Leave Wakkerstroom at 8am and you're in Dullstroom by 1pm. Plenty of afternoon to fish.

📍 Open Day 4 route in Google Maps

Route waypoints (approximate — total ~270 km)

  • Wakkerstroom → Ermelo — ~95 km, 1 hr — rolling highveld, quiet Monday roads
  • Ermelo → Carolina — ~80 km, 55 min — fuel stop in Carolina if needed
  • Carolina → Belfast → Dullstroom — ~95 km, 1 hr — final climb into the Mpumalanga highlands; Dullstroom visible from the R540 descent

Where to stay (2 farm-stay options for 6)

Blue Crane Farm (top pick — boys will love it)

Three 6-sleeper units (Otters Cove, Misty Waters, Weavers Nest) just 50m from the water's edge of a 9-hectare trout dam. Multiple fishing jetties, beginner-friendly fly fishing, full self-catering with fireplaces. The boys can fish off the jetty while you cook supper. ~R4,200/night for 6.

bluecranefarm.co.za →

Millstream Farm

3 km from Dullstroom village. Spacious cottages sleeping up to 10, each with full kitchen, lounge with fireplace, private patio with braai overlooking the trout dams. From ~R2,820/night. South Africa's premier fly-fishing destination.

millstreamfarm.co.za →

Afternoon for the boys

  • Beginner fly-fishing lesson on the dam — most farms hire out rods + offer a quick coaching session
  • Anvil Ale brewery in Dullstroom (kid-safe garden, parents get a beer)
  • Walk the dam wall, build a fire by 4pm — dark by 5:30pm in June
Day 5 Tuesday 23 June

Dullstroom → Hoedspruit via Panorama Route [~230 km]

~230 km ~3.5h driving + 2–3h stops The grand finale

The grand finale

Modest distance, big day of sightseeing. Leave Dullstroom at 7am, climb Long Tom Pass, drop into Sabie for an early lunch, then the Panorama Route's greatest hits: God's Window, Bourke's Luck Potholes, the Three Rondavels viewpoint over the Blyde River Canyon. Drop down through the Strijdom Tunnel into the Lowveld and into Hoedspruit by early evening.

📍 Open Day 5 Panorama Route in Google Maps

Stops, in order

1. Long Tom Pass (Lydenburg → Sabie) — ~1 hr

R37 climbs over 2,150m. Stop at the cannon replica viewpoint. Look for cyclists you can wave at.

2. Sabie — early lunch (11am-ish)

The Wild Fig Tree or Woodsman are reliable. Quick stop, then push on.

3. Mac Mac Falls — 20 min

Roadside viewpoint, no hiking needed. 65m double waterfall.

4. God's Window — 30 min

700m sheer drop over the Lowveld escarpment. Short walk from car park. Spectacular if it's not misted in (June is generally clear).

5. Bourke's Luck Potholes — 1 hr

Cylindrical river-carved potholes where the Blyde meets the Treur. Bridge walks, picnic lawns. R55/adult entry, payable on arrival.

6. Three Rondavels viewpoint — 30 min

The big one. View over Blyde River Canyon (third-largest canyon in the world). Stop at the lookout, take the photos, get back in the car.

7. Strijdom Tunnel → Hoedspruit — 1 hr

Drop ~1,000m down the escarpment through the tunnel. Look for baboons. Arrive Hoedspruit ~5–6pm.

Tactical timing:

  • Hard rule: be on the road by 7am from Dullstroom
  • If running late, the easiest cuts are Mac Mac Falls and God's Window — keep Bourke's Luck and Three Rondavels
  • Sunset in June is ~5:30pm. Don't try to do the Panorama viewpoints in dusk

Practical notes

Pack for cold (this is the big one)

June nights inland in SA are properly cold — Clarens, Dullstroom, Wakkerstroom all routinely drop below 0°C overnight. Daytime is gorgeous (15–22°C, sunny) but the moment the sun drops, it bites.

  • Beanies, gloves, thermal layers for everyone (the boys will need them at Bourke's Luck and Three Rondavels)
  • Hot water bottles or a thermos for the car
  • Down jacket or warm fleece each — fires take a while to warm a stone cottage
  • Closed shoes for farm mud at Funny Stone and the cliff viewpoints on Day 5

Self-catering food strategy

  • Big shop in Grahamstown on Day 1 before leaving — stock up on breakfast (rusks, fruit, yoghurt), snacks, lunch supplies, dinners for Funny Stone
  • Top-up shop in Aliwal North on Day 2 — restock for Clarens and beyond (good Total/Engen with food)
  • Coffee/tea, sugar, salt, oil — most farm stays don't supply pantry basics, double-check at booking
  • Boys love road-trip snack boxes pre-packed (jelly babies, biltong, naartjies)

Fuel strategy

  • Fill in Grahamstown at car pickup (Day 1)
  • Day 1: Queenstown is the main fuel stop en route to Barkly East
  • Day 2: Aliwal North (good Total/Engen with toilets and food — crosses into Free State here)
  • Day 3: Volksrust (bigger town with decent shops — lunch & fuel)
  • Day 4: Carolina if needed, then top up in Dullstroom on arrival
  • Day 5: Top up in Lydenburg before Long Tom Pass, then Sabie if needed

Car prep

  • Twin boys + two adults in the back: agree on a screen-time / book / nap schedule before Day 2 (the long one — 7.5h)
  • USB chargers + power inverter — long days will drain devices
  • Snack tub between the front seats so kids don't have to ask
  • Good road playlist — Spotify offline downloads (parts of the Free State have no signal)

Booking ASAP: June is mid-winter so it's quieter than summer holidays, but Clarens books up for school-holiday weekends and Dullstroom is busy with anglers year-round. Lock in stays at least 4–6 weeks ahead.
