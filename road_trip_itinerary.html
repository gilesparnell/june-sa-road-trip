Trip overview

This is a 4-day road trip from the Eastern Cape coast up to Hoedspruit, designed around rural farm stays, kid-friendly outdoor adventure, and avoiding Johannesburg entirely. The route hand-picks four very different landscapes: mistbelt forest, Free State sandstone country, the highveld trout meccas, and the Panorama Route's spectacular cliffs.

Three trade-offs to know going in:

  • Day 4 is 7–8 hours of driving (Clarens → Dullstroom) — unavoidable given the distance and the no-Joburg constraint. Day 5 is 6–7 hours but broken up by sightseeing stops. Both are best run with a 7am start.
  • June nights inland are COLD (often below freezing in the Berg, Free State, and Mpumalanga highlands). Every recommended stay has a working fireplace — bring beanies, gloves, and a thermos.
  • Self-catering throughout means you can leave at dawn without waiting for breakfast. Pre-pack travel mugs, fruit, and rusks for the road.

Day 1 Friday 19 June

Kenton-on-Sea → Barkly East (Funny Stone) [~450 km]

~450 km 5.5–6 hours driving Into the Berg foothills

The drive

Pick up the hire car in Grahamstown around 8am. Head north-west on the R67 to Fort Beaufort, then north through Adelaide to Queenstown, then south-east through Sterkstroom and Dordrecht as the road climbs steadily into the Berg foothills. Barkly East sits at 1,700m in a spectacular highland valley. Aim to arrive by early-mid afternoon.

⚠️ Do NOT take the Bedford road (R350). The road between Grahamstown and Bedford is chronically in poor condition — widely reported as undrivable. Take the R67 to Fort Beaufort instead. It was fully resurfaced in a R226 million SANRAL project (completed 2020): 70 km of new tar, widened shoulders. Adds ~30 min vs Bedford but is a completely different driving experience.
📍 Open Day 1 route in Google Maps

Route waypoints — take Fort Beaufort, not Bedford

  • Kenton-on-Sea → Grahamstown (Makhanda) — ~80 km, 1 hr — pick up hire car, fill the tank, big shop for Funny Stone days
  • Grahamstown → Fort Beaufort — 70 km, 1 hr — R67 only. Do not take the Bedford road. The R67 is well-maintained resurfaced tar through the Ecca Pass
  • Fort Beaufort → Queenstown (Komani) — 140 km, 1.75 hrs — good fuel & coffee stop in Queenstown before heading into the highlands
  • Queenstown → Dordrecht — 100 km, 1.25 hrs — road begins to climb into the Berg foothills; landscape changes noticeably
  • Dordrecht → Barkly East — 60 km, 1 hr — increasingly spectacular as you enter the highlands at 1,700m

Where you're staying — Funny Stone

Funny Stone (family)

Family property in Barkly East. Arrive, unpack, light a fire. You've earned it — the rest of the trip is from here.

If you arrive with energy

  • Walk the farm — the boys will want to explore before dark
  • Barkly East village is small but has a good butchery — grab boerewors for the braai
  • Sunset at altitude hits fast in June — fire on by 4:30pm
Day 2 Saturday 20 June

Rest day — Funny Stone, Barkly East [no driving]

No driving required Explore the southern Berg Longest day of the year ☀️

Day options — pick one or mix

1. Naude's Nek Pass drive (must-do if weather is clear)

One of the highest mountain passes in South Africa, topping out at ~2,590m above sea level. About 70 km east of Barkly East on a gravel road — doable in a standard vehicle in dry conditions. The views over the Drakensberg escarpment are extraordinary. Allow a half-day, bring a flask.

2. Rhodes village

~65 km from Barkly East through spectacular mountain scenery. Tiny, perfectly preserved village in a high valley — stone cottages, trout streams, the classic Walkerbouts Inn for lunch. The boys can fish the Bokspruit River while the adults have a glass of something warm.

3. Ben McDhui Ski Resort

~40 km from Barkly East near Rhodes — the southernmost ski resort in Africa. June can bring snow to the upper slopes. Even without snow, the drive up and the views are worth it. Check conditions before you go.

4. Just be at Funny Stone

A long braai lunch, a walk on the farm, card games in the afternoon, a proper fire in the evening. The road trip proper starts tomorrow — this is the decompression day.

Note: 20 June is the winter solstice — the shortest day of the year, but clear June days in the Berg are famously beautiful. Cold at night (can drop well below 0°C), warm and sunny from about 9am–4pm.

Day 3 Sunday 21 June

Barkly East → Clarens (Eastern Free State) [~340 km]

~340 km 4 hours driving Manageable — arrive with energy

The drive

The road from Barkly East to Clarens is genuinely beautiful. Head north-west through the Klein Drakensberg foothills via Lady Grey, cross the Orange River into the Free State at Aliwal North, then track north through sandstone Free State farmland to Clarens. Leave after a slow breakfast — no need for a dawn start today.

Route waypoints

  • Barkly East → Lady Grey — 60 km, 1 hr — lovely foothills road, one of the prettier stretches of the trip
  • Lady Grey → Aliwal North — 80 km, 1 hr — crosses into the Free State at the Orange River; good fuel stop here
  • Aliwal North → Zastron → Wepener — 130 km, 1.5 hrs — flat Free State farmland, easy driving

Where to stay (2 farm-stay options for 6)

The Barbet Country Cottages (top pick)

Partly thatched 3-bedroom cottage on a large farm 17 km from Clarens along the scenic Maluti route. Cosy sunroom with fireplace, fully equipped kitchen, sleeps 6+1. Big farm feel, space for the boys to run. ~R655 pp/night (≈ R3,930/night for 6). Exactly the vibe — thatched, fires, farm.

Day 3 Alt Sunday 21 June

Barkly East → Wakkerstroom (via KZN) [~455 km]

~455 km 5.5 hours driving Cuts Day 4 to just 3.5h

Instead of stopping at Clarens, take the KZN route north — trading the Free State sandstone country for KwaZulu-Natal farmland and arriving in Wakkerstroom. The payoff: Day 4 to Dullstroom shrinks from 7–8 hours to 3.5 hours. You give up Clarens; you gain a sane Monday.

The drive

Head north-west from Barkly East as usual via Lady Grey and Aliwal North, then swing north-east into KZN — Ladysmith, Newcastle, Volksrust — and drop 35 km south-east to Wakkerstroom. All tar, quiet roads, progressively more open highveld as you climb.

Route waypoints

  • Barkly East → Lady Grey → Aliwal North — 140 km, 1.5 hrs — fuel & coffee stop in Aliwal North
  • Aliwal North → Ladysmith — 140 km, 1.5 hrs — enters KZN; landscape opens up onto the highveld
  • Ladysmith → Newcastle — 100 km, 1 hr — lunch stop in Newcastle if needed
  • Newcastle → Volksrust — 40 km, 30 min
  • Volksrust → Wakkerstroom — 35 km, 30 min — drop south-east off the highway into the village

Trade-off: This route bypasses Clarens and the Golden Gate sandstone country entirely. If Clarens is on your must-see list, stick with the main route. If a humane Day 4 matters more, take this one.

Where to stay — Wakkerstroom

Wakkerstroom is a tiny, beautiful highveld village at 1,650 m — renowned as one of SA's top birding destinations. Cold, clear June nights, big skies, good fires.

Wakkerstroom Farm Lodge (Saxony Farm) (top pick)

6 km from town on the Newcastle / Zaaihoek dam road. ~8 km of dam frontage. Birding, walking, mountain biking and bass fishing on site, kayaks for the dam. Sleeps 6+.

Day 4 Monday 22 June

Clarens → Dullstroom [~620 km]

~620 km 7–8 hours driving ⚠ Long day — 7am start

The drive — the no-Joburg route

This is the day you skirt above Joburg. Route runs Clarens → Bethlehem → Vrede → Volksrust (KZN/Mpumalanga border) → Standerton → Ermelo → Carolina → Belfast → Dullstroom. All tar, mostly quiet roads through farmland. Leave by 7am, lunch in Volksrust or Standerton, arrive Dullstroom mid-afternoon.

Route waypoints

  • Clarens → Bethlehem → Vrede — 200 km, 2.5 hrs — flat Free State farming country, good early morning driving
  • Vrede → Volksrust — 175 km, 2 hrs — lunch & fuel stop in Volksrust; this is the KZN/Mpumalanga border crossing, decent shops
  • Volksrust → Ermelo → Carolina — 200 km, 2.5 hrs — highveld grassland, progressively more scenic as Carolina approaches
  • Carolina → Belfast → Dullstroom — 110 km, 1.25 hrs — final push into the Mpumalanga highlands; Dullstroom visible from the R540 descent

Where to stay (2 farm-stay options for 6)

Blue Crane Farm (top pick — boys will love it)

Three 6-sleeper units (Otters Cove, Misty Waters, Weavers Nest) just 50m from the water's edge of a 9-hectare trout dam. Multiple fishing jetties, beginner-friendly fly fishing, full self-catering with fireplaces. The boys can fish off the jetty while you cook supper. ~R4,200/night for 6.

bluecranefarm.co.za →

Millstream Farm

3 km from Dullstroom village. Spacious cottages sleeping up to 10, each with full kitchen, lounge with fireplace, private patio with braai overlooking the trout dams. From ~R2,820/night. South Africa's premier fly-fishing destination.

millstreamfarm.co.za →

Afternoon for the boys

  • Beginner fly-fishing lesson on the dam — most farms hire out rods + offer a quick coaching session
  • Anvil Ale brewery in Dullstroom (kid-safe garden, parents get a beer)
  • Walk the dam wall, build a fire by 4pm — dark by 5:30pm in June
Day 5 Tuesday 23 June

Dullstroom → Hoedspruit via Panorama Route [~270 km]

~270 km 6–7 hours with sightseeing stops The grand finale

The grand finale

Modest distance, big day of sightseeing. Leave Dullstroom at 7am, climb Long Tom Pass, drop into Sabie for an early lunch, then the Panorama Route's greatest hits: God's Window, Bourke's Luck Potholes, the Three Rondavels viewpoint over the Blyde River Canyon. Drop down through the Strijdom Tunnel into the Lowveld and into Hoedspruit by early evening.

📍 Open Day 5 Panorama Route in Google Maps

Stops, in order

1. Long Tom Pass (Lydenburg → Sabie) — ~1 hr

R37 climbs over 2,150m. Stop at the cannon replica viewpoint. Look for cyclists you can wave at.

2. Sabie — early lunch (11am-ish)

The Wild Fig Tree or Woodsman are reliable. Quick stop, then push on.

3. Mac Mac Falls — 20 min

Roadside viewpoint, no hiking needed. 65m double waterfall.

4. God's Window — 30 min

700m sheer drop over the Lowveld escarpment. Short walk from car park. Spectacular if it's not misted in (June is generally clear).

5. Bourke's Luck Potholes — 1 hr

Cylindrical river-carved potholes where the Blyde meets the Treur. Bridge walks, picnic lawns. R55/adult entry, payable on arrival.

6. Three Rondavels viewpoint — 30 min

The big one. View over Blyde River Canyon (third-largest canyon in the world). Stop at the lookout, take the photos, get back in the car.

7. Strijdom Tunnel → Hoedspruit — 1 hr

Drop ~1,000m down the escarpment through the tunnel. Look for baboons. Arrive Hoedspruit ~5–6pm.

Tactical timing:

  • Hard rule: be on the road by 7am from Dullstroom
  • If running late, the easiest cuts are Mac Mac Falls and God's Window — keep Bourke's Luck and Three Rondavels
  • Sunset in June is ~5:30pm. Don't try to do the Panorama viewpoints in dusk

Practical notes

Pack for cold (this is the big one)

June nights inland in SA are properly cold — Clarens, Dullstroom, Wakkerstroom all routinely drop below 0°C overnight. Daytime is gorgeous (15–22°C, sunny) but the moment the sun drops, it bites.

  • Beanies, gloves, thermal layers for everyone (the boys will need them at Bourke's Luck and Three Rondavels)
  • Hot water bottles or a thermos for the car
  • Down jacket or warm fleece each — fires take a while to warm a stone cottage
  • Closed shoes for farm mud at Funny Stone and the cliff viewpoints on Day 5

Self-catering food strategy

  • Big shop in Grahamstown on Day 1 before leaving — stock up on breakfast (rusks, fruit, yoghurt), snacks, lunch supplies, dinners for Funny Stone
  • Top-up shop in Barkly East village or Aliwal North on Day 3 — restock for Clarens and beyond
  • Coffee/tea, sugar, salt, oil — most farm stays don't supply pantry basics, double-check at booking
  • Boys love road-trip snack boxes pre-packed (jelly babies, biltong, naartjies)

Fuel strategy

  • Fill in Grahamstown at car pickup (Day 1)
  • Day 1: Queenstown is the main fuel stop en route to Barkly East
  • Day 3: Aliwal North (good Total/Engen with toilets and food — crosses into Free State here)
  • Day 4: Volksrust (bigger town, better stops than Vrede)
  • Day 5: Top up in Lydenburg before Long Tom Pass, then Sabie if needed

Car prep

  • Twin boys + two adults in the back: agree on a screen-time / book / nap schedule before Day 4 (the long one)
  • USB chargers + power inverter — long days will drain devices
  • Snack tub between the front seats so kids don't have to ask
  • Good road playlist — Spotify offline downloads (parts of the Free State have no signal)

Booking ASAP: June is mid-winter so it's quieter than summer holidays, but Clarens books up for school-holiday weekends and Dullstroom is busy with anglers year-round. Lock in stays at least 4–6 weeks ahead.
